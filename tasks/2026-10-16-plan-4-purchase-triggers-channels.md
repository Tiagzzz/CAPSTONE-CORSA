# Plan 4: Behavioural purchase triggers and acquisition channels by age group (Jakarta)

Date: 2026-10-16. Author: planner-4. Status: DRAFT for owner approval. No data has been collected; the source scan below only checked that sources exist.
Feeds: Tiago's experiential-marketing and launch strategy; gap analysis gaps 7 (acquisition and community) and 9 (cultural and brand transfer); bias guards B1, B2, B7, B14 (`reports/2026-10-16-existing-research-gap-analysis.md` §6).
Out of scope: CAC modelling (flag any CAC found as a **Team B input**), pricing and willingness to pay (plan 1), halal regulation (plan 2), competitor map (plan 3). Halal is covered here only as a consumer venue-choice factor.

## 1. Goal, decisions, research questions

**Decisions it feeds:** (D1) which age group(s) the launch should be designed for; (D2) which channel mix to test first (creators, run/padel clubs, pop-up events, delivery apps); (D3) which CORSA identity elements to lead with at entry, soften or drop (racing, Italian, community, functional drinks); (D4) what the team must test by primary research before spending launch budget.

| # | Research question (testable) |
| --- | --- |
| Q1 | Which platforms do urban Indonesians (Jakarta where possible) use for discovery and purchase, by generation, and is TikTok/Instagram discovery measurably ahead of friends, Google Maps and delivery apps for food venues? |
| Q2 | What share of urban consumers say they try new F&B concepts or viral products, and how does this differ by generation? Is novelty a trigger for first visit only, or also for repeat? (proxy: FOMO and viral-venue studies) |
| Q3 | How much do health and wellness motives change actual purchases (stated vs observed), by generation? Is there a gap between "want to eat healthy" and what is bought? |
| Q4 | How far do consumers trust KOLs, creators, friends and brand content, by generation, and is trust falling for large influencers (mega vs micro)? How large is live and social commerce for food? |
| Q5 | How big and how young are running, padel and fitness communities in Jakarta, and is there evidence they act as a purchase or venue-choice channel (not only a hobby)? |
| Q6 | How do consumers evaluate foreign, Western and Italian or European brands against local brands (country-of-origin, ethnocentrism, "local pride"), by generation? How durable was the 2023-2025 boycott effect, and which brand types were hit? |
| Q7 | How important are "third place" and experiential venues (dwell time, social use) and halal/religious considerations when choosing a venue, by generation? |
| Q8 | Is there any Indonesian audience evidence on racing/motorsport (MotoGP, F1, Ducati) and on Italian food or lifestyle that supports or undermines the racing and Italian identity for the target segments? |

## 2. Hypotheses with confirming and disconfirming evidence

| # | Hypothesis (to be tested, not assumed) | Confirming evidence | Disconfirming evidence (what would force a change) | If disconfirmed, change at entry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | Social discovery (TikTok/Instagram, creators) is the main first-visit trigger for 25-39 urban consumers | Social platforms rank above friends/Maps/offline for F&B discovery in at least two independent sources, in the 25-39 bands | Friends/word-of-mouth or promos lead; 29+ cohorts rely mainly on Google Maps/delivery apps; trust in creators falling | Shift budget from creator seeding to peer referral, Maps/review presence, delivery-app promos |
| H2 | Novelty and viral appeal trigger trial, especially for Gen Z and younger Millennials | Studies show FOMO/novelty significantly linked to purchase intention in Jakarta F&B; younger bands score higher | Novelty effects are small, only for trial with weak repeat; older Millennials/Gen X score low; "viral" venues show short life | Position on repeatable routine (coffee, lunch), not novelty; do not rely on launch buzz |
| H3 | Health motivation is real but changes purchases only modestly and mostly at constant price | Stated health intent high AND observed or price-capped behaviour consistent (NIQ: only 19% pay 20% more) | Health is a top-3 choice driver with observed uplift in Jakarta urban segments | Lead with taste/convenience and "balanced"; treat health as secondary claim |
| H4 | Run clubs and padel communities are a viable acquisition channel for the 25-39 core | Large and growing participation; clubs meet at cafés; skew 25-44; sources show clubs influence venue/product choice | Participation concentrated in a small elite or in other age bands; no evidence of purchase influence; community is Strava/gear-led, not F&B-led | Treat community as brand-building, not acquisition; lean on delivery and office-lunch channels |
| H5 | Italian/European origin is a mild positive or neutral signal | Origin linked to quality/innovation for Gen Z in Indonesian COO studies; boycotts concentrated on brands tied to specific conflicts, not "European" in general | Ethnocentrism/local-pride effects significant; boycott durable and generalising to foreign F&B; Italian = "expensive, not for daily meals" | Lead with product and local partnerships; de-emphasise "foreign import"; pre-empt political framing |
| H6 | Racing identity resonates with the target | Motorsport (MotoGP, F1) audience in Jakarta is young, urban and sizeable; overlaps with café/run segment | Motorsport audience is male/regional/different SES or age; no overlap with target; racing reads as "fast food" or masculine-niche | Make racing a secondary design/story layer, not the lead message |
| H7 | Functional drinks / community / "third place" transfer | Third-place use (hours, groups, work) documented; functional-drink uptake rising | Sugar/label rules or health scepticism dominate; groups prefer existing cafés | Keep drinks in a supporting role; test menu before building a drinks-led concept |
| H8 | Halal cues influence venue choice for Muslim majority | Studies show halal certification linked to intent; high Muslim share | Effect small or absent among young urban cohorts; or large and decisive | Large: certification is entry condition (link to plan 2). Small: still required by law, but do not lead with it |

## 4. Segmentation and age-band harmonisation

Sources use different cuts: APJII 2025 = Gen Z 13-28, Millennial 29-44, Gen X, Boomers; Jakpat/Populix = 15-25, 18-24, 25-34 style bands; PwC and NIQ = Gen Z/Millennial/Gen X labels; Strava = generation labels; academic studies = "Gen Z" or ranges.

1. **Canonical bands (by birth year, as of 2026):** Gen Z 18-28 (born 1998-2008; under-18s excluded), Younger Millennials 29-35, Older Millennials 36-45, Gen X 46-61. Tiago's 25-39 core straddles Gen Z/Younger/Older Millennials, so the matrix reports the core as a derived row ("25-39 where available").
2. **Crosswalk table** (`data/processed/age-band-crosswalk.csv`): for each source record its native band, the nearest canonical band, and an overlap code: A = exact fit, B = overlaps >75%, C = overlaps 50-75%, D = <50% or mixed. Cells with D are shown but flagged and never aggregated. No interpolation or re-weighting of published percentages; if the band does not map, report it as published.
3. **Target segments:** use the gap-analysis segment labels (A young CBD professionals 25-39, B residents, C sport/community, D students, E expats, F other) only to tag samples; no segment weights are invented. Where a source reports occupation, income class (SES A/B) or city, record it as segment evidence.
4. **Geography labels:** J = Jakarta specific, U = national urban, N = national, P = proxy category (other product category) or proxy country. Each cell carries both an age-fit code and a geography label.

## 10. Risks, effort, order of work (interim deadline 21/22 Oct)

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Many "Jakarta + age" cells will be empty because sources are national and bands differ | Accept GAP cells; report proxy distance; do not interpolate |
