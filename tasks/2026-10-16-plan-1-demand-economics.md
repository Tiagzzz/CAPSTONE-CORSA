## 1. Goal, decisions fed, research questions

**Goal.** Replace the unsourced demand inputs in the financial model (200 guests/day, no visit-frequency input, IDR 103k average spend; MOD `03-inputs.csv`) with sourced, labelled ranges, and test whether the target segment (25-39, high / upper-middle income, SCBD-Senopati-Kuningan) is large and solvent enough.

**Decisions fed.** (a) Keep, narrow or change the target segment (CBR/TC segment A). (b) Price position: "premium but accessible" vs "middle, affordable" (T4, T18). (c) Plausible guests/day range for Team B's conservative / base / optimistic cases. (d) Whether the "growing middle class" reason for Jakarta (SCOPE section 2) stays in the interim presentation.

## 2. Hypotheses with confirming and disconfirming criteria

| ID | Hypothesis (current team view) | Confirmed if (sourced) | Disconfirmed if (sourced) | If disconfirmed, the team should |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Target pool is large enough for one flagship | Funnel (Q9) central case gives at least the model's break-even of 128 guests/day, with a plausible capture rate | Central case below 128 guests/day, or the pool must be taken from many districts beyond a 15-min catchment | Reduce format / seats, lower the volume case in the model, add a delivery / hybrid format or a second daypart, and say so in the interim deck |
| H2 | "Growing middle class" supports Jakarta | Official series shows the Jakarta (not national) middle / upper class stable or growing 2019-latest | National and Jakarta series both fall (the team's own 57.3M to 46.7M) | Drop the claim from the scope wording; restate "a shrinking but concentrated upper tier"; target the top tier only |
| H3 | Segment A can afford more than one premium meal a week | Median spend of upper-class Jakarta households on prepared food covers a IDR 85-110k bowl 4+ times a month (the bowl is 2.2-2.9x average daily food spend per gap analysis row 89) | Upper-class prepared-food spend cannot cover 4 bowls a month (thresholds are working criteria set by the planner, not sourced; the owner may adjust before execution) | Move price band down (core bowl and coffee lower), shift from weekly to occasional / social occasion, test a value line |
| H4 | Healthy / specialty coffee earns a price premium | Several independent sources show a WTP of 20% or more for a defined group (e.g. urban upper income) | Public data stays at 10% or lower (NIQ: 19% would pay 20% more) and the Jakarta café price ladder shows no premium for "healthy" | Price against the competitive set, not on a "health premium"; sell on taste, community and experience |
| H5 | Visit frequency is weekly or better for the core | Revealed data (spend per visit and monthly café spend) imply 4+ visits a month for the target | Data imply 1-2 visits a month | Rebuild volume on a larger, less frequent base (residents, tourists, events), or smaller format |
| H6 | Lunch and dinner dominate; mornings matter less | Daypart sources show lunch + evening at or above 65% of occasions | Afternoon coffee and morning dominate, or delivery above 40% | Rebalance the daypart mix (cafe-led format, delivery kitchen), revisit seat count |
| H7 | Rice norm is a barrier to pasta | Wheat-food consumption is flat / falling, chains with pasta show no presence in target districts | Wheat consumption and imports for food are rising and pasta / noodle chains are present and expanding (USDA, BPS, listed chains) | Retire the rice-norm risk from the top of the ranking; keep a grain-bowl option as a test, not a necessity |
| H8 | 200 guests/day is attainable | Public sales-per-store data of comparable chains imply 150+ guests/day | Leading chain average implies around 100 or fewer guests/day (model already shows 1.9x Fore) | Pass a lower volume range to Team B; make the conservative case the planning case |

Disconfirming searches are mandatory work items (WP-D below), not optional extras.

## 4. Segmentation and harmonisation

- **Age bands:** 15-24, **25-29, 30-34, 35-39 (target 25-39)**, 40-49, 50+. BPS population projections use 5-year bands: use those as the master grid. Cohort labels (Gen Z, Millennial) are mapped to bands, never used alone (gap analysis row 6).
- **Income / expenditure classes (master: BPS / World Bank per-capita expenditure classes):** poor, vulnerable, aspiring middle, middle, upper. Cross-walk: Bank Indonesia consumer-survey monthly expenditure bands (IDR 1-2M ... above 5M, household level); SES A / B / C classes used by commercial surveys; Jakpat self-reported income bands. Per-capita and per-household figures are never mixed: convert with the BPS average household size, state it in the data README, and keep the original in `data/raw`.
- **Target definition for the funnel (to agree before data collection, one line in the data README):** "age 25-39, in upper or middle class by per-capita expenditure, living or working in the SCBD-Senopati-Kuningan catchment". Because Susenas stops at city level, the catchment is a **share assumption** on top of South Jakarta / Central Jakarta totals (flagged as analysis, with low / central / high).
- **Segments A-F (CBR):** only A (CBD professionals), B (residents) and D (students) can be sized from public data; C (sports communities) and E / F only qualitatively. The scores are not reused.
- **Reference years:** every figure carries its reference year; mixed years are never combined without CPI-deflation or a note (BPS CPI).

## 5. Proxy strategy and limits

| Proxy | Distance to target | Use | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| BPS city spend (South Jakarta mean) for the SCBD-Senopati micro-catchment | Mean includes all residents, not 25-39 professionals; understates the upper tier | Upper bound on the average, lower bound for the top tier | Means hide the tail; use expenditure classes and not just means |
| National middle-class counts for Jakarta | National, class definition by expenditure | Direction of trend only | Jakarta share unknown; flag |
| Jakpat (age 15-25 or 20-35, online, Java) for age 25-39 | Younger, online, not Jakarta-only | Frequency and daypart, flagged as proxy | Self-selected online panels; vendor tier 3 |
| NIQ national grocery WTP for restaurant markups | Grocery, all ages | Ceiling signal only | Cannot set a restaurant price |
| Listed chain sales per store for CORSA's volume | Chains differ in format, price and location | Order of magnitude only | Aggregate, not in-district |
| Wheat / noodle national statistics for pasta acceptance | Staple wheat use, not restaurant pasta | Acceptance direction | Does not show taste or repeat purchase |
| Sensitivity to innovation as a purchase trigger (e.g. new-product intent in Indonesian consumer surveys, Jakpat, dentsu) | Generic | Supporting only, never a stand-alone figure | Must carry sample and year |

Every proxy row in the datasets gets columns `proxy_type` and `distance_note`. Rule: a proxy may widen or narrow a range but never supplies a central CORSA figure alone.

## 7. Verification (CLAUDE.md section 7)

V1 and V2 are separate from the authors. V1 samples every figure feeding Team B plus at least 30% of the others; re-opens the cited page, checks value, unit, year, population and context; separates the republisher from the primary and requires the primary where it exists (Databoks to BPS; press to the World Bank report). Mismatch means the figure is dropped to the gap log until fixed. V2 checks each source has all fields and a justified tier, links resolve (archive URL noted), tier-4 only claims are flagged and carry no figure, and the freshness rule (>= 2024-10-16 or a long-run series with a recent last point) holds. The verifier also confirms no paid-only content was used or copied.

## 9. Gaps desk research will not fill, and primary-research questions
