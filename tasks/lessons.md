## 2026-10-16 — Snippet-only collection: errors caught by verification
- **What happened:** In run 1 (hosts blocked, figures taken from search results), independent verifiers found errors or unreproducible figures in about one fifth of the rows: 18 rows were dropped (UNCONFIRMED, DERIVED, duplicate, AUDIT-DISPUTED) and about 20 corrected or downgraded.
- **Error types:** (1) misread subset or category: 6.87% "strongly trust" treated as part of 59.61% "trust" when the source lists them as separate categories; (2) period mix-ups: nine months reported as Q3, a quarter's growth (148.5%) read as full-year, a preliminary one-month count (359) treated as final, wrong qualifier "lowest since September 2025" (June, not July); (3) dates inferred from URL paths or hex IDs instead of read from the page, and survey fieldwork dates confused with publication dates; (4) single-outlet reliance: several figures only appeared in one outlet (Bisnis.com, GoodStats, Tirto) or in republishers of one press text, so a second search was not independent; (5) conflicting headline vs body (Bisnis said GrabFood for Millennials, the body said GoFood; Databoks printed lunch 23% where other sources say 37%); also wrong cited URL (a different Bisnis article than the one carrying the figure), mistyped percentages (-3.22 vs -3.12), and worker arithmetic stored as a data point.
- **Preventive rules:** (a) Record the exact category label and its base for every percentage and check whether categories are additive or nested. (b) State the period type (quarter, 9M, FY, month) in the value cell and never infer it from a headline. (c) Take dates from a page or a second outlet; label URL-inferred dates as such; record fieldwork and publication dates separately. (d) A confirmation counts as independent only if it comes from a different publisher that does not republish the same press text; otherwise mark the row weak. (e) When headline and body disagree, store both and the conflict, and do not pick one without the original. (f) Do not store derived values (differences, durations, sums) as data rows; put them in notes with the formula. (g) Copy the URL of the article that carries the figure, not a related one. (h) Keep snippet-only figures flagged provisional until the source is opened.

## 2026-10-17 — Case-table worker errors (foreign entrants)
- **What happened:** The run-2 verifier of `foreign-entrants.csv` (`tasks/2026-10-17-run2-verification-e.md`) corrected 5 of 29 rows, blocked 1, and failed 6 on the date rule. Errors: (1) the P3B-02 date came from unrelated sidebar tags (2026-09-30, 2026-10-07) instead of the article (28 Apr 2026); (2) six pre-window rows (printed 2023 to Jul 2024) were kept in the CSV with a stale class instead of being dropped; (3) paraphrases were treated as quotes (the Starbucks boycott reason is the journalist's paraphrase of the CEO; "about Rp 50,000" added to a "kisaran" range; Mixue 428 closures given a scope no outlet stated); (4) "disconfirmed" claims for Domino's and Pizza Marzano rested on sources that never name Indonesia or on one old opening.
- **Cause:** Dates were taken from page metadata or links outside the article body; the date rule was applied to the row class and not to the printed source date; the worker wrote reasons without checking whether the sentence was a quote; negative or adjacent evidence was promoted to a finding.
- **Preventive rules:** (a) Take a publication date only from the article body or its own metadata block, never from sidebar, related-article or "read also" links; if two dates appear, check which belongs to the article. (b) Apply the date rule to the printed source date of every row; a row whose only source is pre-window goes to the dropped-rows file and into the raw findings under "Historical / pre-window context", not into the CSV. (c) Put a statement in `reasons_fact` as a quote only if the article puts it in quotation marks as the speaker's words; otherwise label it "reported by the outlet, paraphrasing X", and copy range wording ("kisaran", "mulai dari") exactly without adding "about". (d) Never write "disconfirmed" unless an opened source names Indonesia and the specific claim; otherwise write "no exit evidence found" with the date of the last check, and mark the row provisional if it rests on a blocked page. (e) Outlets that relay one original report (Detik, CNBC, Suara on VnExpress) count as one source; record the scope each outlet states.


## 2026-10-17 — Legal/price worker errors
- **What happened:** Run-3 verifiers (`tasks/2026-10-17-run3-verification-{a,b,c}.md`) corrected 15 of 171 rows and flagged four error types. (1) Wrong article numbers: T-05 cited Art. 117 for the revocation of Perda 11/2011 (it is Art. 116(i)); F-09 cited Art. 5 for the effective date (it is Art. 8); a T-08 clause on Art. 31E pro-rata went beyond what the cited DJP deck says. (2) Unopened or derived figures leaked into value cells: S-10 carried "+17.3% per Warta Ekonomi (not opened)", S-04 carried a derived "remainder 7.0", and the S-04 notes inferred a base for S-05 (the 89% is a substitution preference, not boycott participation; the register mislabelled it). (3) Approximate prices (sekitar, -an, ribuan, Google Maps, ordering app) were counted as exact list prices in the summary statistics, and a promo-article "normal price" (P3PB-45) was assigned to one item the page did not name; fruit Americanos were counted as milk-coffee. (4) Brand concentration in the price ladder: Burgreens 36%, Pizza Marzano 27% and Fore Coffee 21% of numeric rows, so the plan's 15% cap was missed and category medians were medians of one to three brands.
- **Cause:** Article numbers were written from memory and not matched to the opened text; the worker put unopened or computed values in value/unit cells instead of notes; the price table had no way to mark approximate prices; collection stopped at the first brands that had reachable pages; tier-4 or relay sources were used where an official host (DJP country page, SIF, BKPM) existed.
- **Preventive rules:** (a) Copy every article number from the opened instrument (quote the heading line) and never from memory; check revocation and effective-date articles by reading the closing articles. (b) Never put an unopened source, a derived value or a remainder in a value or unit cell; move it to notes with the formula or leave it out (extends the 2026-10-16 rule f). (c) State a scope or base only if the cited source states it; do not infer one row's base from another row. (d) Flag every price given as sekitar, -an, ribuan, Google Maps or app as approximate in a dedicated column and report an "approx" count beside each statistic; promo-article "normal prices" are not item prices unless the page names the item. (e) Classify items by ingredients (milk or no milk) before pooling them into a class. (f) Report each brand's share of numeric rows next to the price statistics, collect at least four brands per category before computing a median, and mark cells dominated by one brand. (g) Search the publisher's own site (DJP, BKPM, SIF, USDA original) before accepting a relay or a mirror.

## 2026-10-17 — Time-limited rules worded as current; audit checkers missing context columns
- **What happened:** The main-session audit (`tasks/2026-10-17-main-session-audit.md`) found H-03 describing the 2025-only SEHATI free halal scheme in the present tense. Separately, the audit's own checkers flagged T-06, P1C1-F04 and P1C2-22 as stale because they were given value and quote columns but not `notes`, `in_force_confirmation` or `amendment_check`, where the rows already handled the point.
- **Cause:** The FAQ answer's period ("sepanjang tahun 2025") was not carried into the finding. The audit sample omitted the context columns.
- **Preventive rules:** (a) When a source describes a programme, discount or decree with a time limit, write its period into the finding and check that it is still running on the date of writing. (b) Any verifier or auditor gets the full row, including notes, in-force and amendment columns, and re-reads it before calling a row stale.

## 2026-10-17 — Synthesis reports: balance and traceability slips (run 4)
- **What happened:** Independent verifiers of the Pillar 1 reports (profiles, CAGE, PESTEL; `tasks/2026-10-17-run4-verification-*.md`) found no wrong figure but needed two to three rounds each. Recurring defects: (1) the "facts pointing the other way" section claimed to cite all verified downside rows but missed 10 to 25 each time; (2) an analysis step shown as fact with high confidence (H-03: "a PMA counts as large for halal"); (3) a confidence level in the report higher than the row's own level (H-07, H-12); (4) flight durations taken from the wrong direction (the 17h35 flights are the return); (5) a gap row that said a host was unusable when it worked via another path (JDIH Jakarta search); (6) trimming a report for length silently dropped a table row, tags and an article number; (7) groups defined by savings balance labelled "middle class"; (8) two writers appended to the same register, so one worker edited its own register lines in place.
- **Cause:** The writers built the risk section from memory of the disconfirming file and not from a scripted list; labels were copied between rows; length cuts were not diffed against the earlier version.
- **Preventive rules:** (a) Build the downside list by script from the disconfirming file and from every mapped row whose notes or direction say downside or mixed, and say the selection rule in the section; record the counts. (b) Copy confidence from the row, never raise it; a step that is not in a legal text is labelled Analysis and given at most medium. (c) For travel times, quote the direction and the flight numbers. (d) Before writing "host unusable", try the publisher's own search path and one other route. (e) After any length trim, diff the new version against the old for dropped table rows, tags and article numbers. (f) Use the label the source uses for a group (savings balance, expenditure class); do not translate it. (g) Parallel workers take register ids from separate ranges (REG-045+, REG-100+, REG-131+); each edits only its own lines.
