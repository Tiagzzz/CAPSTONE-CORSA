  None of the three is validated yet.

2. Concept
CORSA, a Swiss brand, would open a flagship in Jakarta to test its format in Indonesia: an all-day
venue serving specialty coffee and healthy Mediterranean food. The working case is a 120 to 160 m²
unit with 60 seats, open 07:00–22:00, selling dine-in, take-away and delivery. Prices are pitched at
the smart-casual level of South Jakarta's lifestyle districts (see section 3).

3. Location options

Working case highlighted. Occupancy benchmark: rent and service charge at 8–10% of sales [5, 6]. Demand levels are team judgements.

SCBD needs its assumed 10% extra demand just to match the main road, because its rent and
service charge are about twice as high. Senopati side streets trade an assumed 10% less demand
for 56% lower rent. Kemang and Kuningan fall behind mainly on lower assumed demand, and
Kuningan also on rent. PIK looks strongest on paper, but it is Tier 2 and outside the zone used for
competitor pricing, so its demand level is the least tested.
The ranking rests on the demand column and on thin rent samples outside Kemang and the Senopati
side streets. Footfall counts and the district scoring, planned with gravity models [7, 8], will decide
between them.
The rent evidence is 30 asking-rent listings of 100–200 m² from Jakarta property portals, collected 6–
8 Oct 2026 [9–20]. Deals usually close 5–15% lower. Senopati main road has one listing, an upper
floor at about 289k per m² [16], so the model estimates it as side-street rent times a 2.27 main-road
premium. Mall space is much dearer: IDR 564k per m² plus IDR 154k service charge on
average [21], in a retail market where occupancy is tightening [22, 23]. Shop-house (ruko) leases
typically run at least 12 months, paid 1–2 years upfront [9, 10].

6. Investment and funding
The Base investment of IDR 5.09bn (CHF 236k) is half build-out and half cash tied up before and
after opening. Rent paid upfront and the working-capital reserve together account for IDR 1.16bn
(CHF 54k) [1].

Working-capital reserve      0.70bn   33k    3 months of fixed costs excluding rent
Total                        5.09bn   236k



The CAPEX is based on unit-cost estimates, not quotes. Three equipment prices disagree with
dealer list prices converted to IDR [64–68]: the combi oven looks about 50% too high, the espresso
machine about 20% too low and the grinders about 35% too low. Leasing part of the equipment is
being evaluated as a way to cut the upfront amount.
Funding the gap. The CHF 200k equity (IDR 4.32bn) leaves IDR 772m (CHF 36k) to find. The
model assumes a 48-month loan at 10.5%, based on the prime lending rate plus a risk premium [69,
70]. Local banks rarely lend to a newly formed foreign-owned company without collateral, so a loan
from Switzerland to the parent, or a shareholder loan, is the more realistic route.
Legal structure is the bigger question. A foreign-owned company (PT PMA) needs IDR 2.5bn
(CHF 116k) of paid-up capital per business line, which the equity covers. It must also declare an
investment plan of more than IDR 10bn (CHF 463k) per business line, about twice this project [2, 3].
Unless that plan can be met over time with more outlets, alternatives such as a local partner holding
a CORSA licence or franchise, or a joint venture, may be needed. As a PT PMA the company would
pay standard corporate tax, not the 0.5% small-business rate [71], and must withhold 10% tax on
rent [72]. This rests on BKPM Regulation 5/2025 as described by consultancy sources and has not
been confirmed by a lawyer.
