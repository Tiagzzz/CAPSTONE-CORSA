## 02 Culture

### Collective and hierarchical: test the rice norm

**WHY WE LOOKED** Meeting 4 asked whether Indonesia's collectivism is a threat or an advantage [9], and the scope asks whether the brand transfers culturally.

**THE DATA** Hofstede's cultural dimensions (Hofstede, 1991 [2]); scores 0 to 100 from The Culture Factor [1], with Paul's note per dimension (6 Oct 2026). Scores are national averages, not Jakarta customers; the Philippines and Switzerland are for comparison only.

| 38 | indulgence, Indonesia (Switzerland 66) [1] |

| Dimension | Indonesia | Philippines | Switzerland | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Individualism | 5 | 17 | 79 | RELEVANT: RICE NORM |

Research verdict legend: RELEVANT, CHECK, SET ASIDE.

Hofstede scores 0 to 100, Indonesia / Philippines / Switzerland: power distance 78 / 94 / 34; individualism 5 / 17 / 79; motivation towards achievement and success 46 / 64 / 70; uncertainty avoidance 48 / 44 / 58; long-term orientation 29 / 46 / 42; indulgence 38 / 42 / 66 [1].

**WHAT MATTERS FOR CUSTOMERS [1]**

- Individualism: Indonesia 5, Philippines 17, Switzerland 79: extremely collectivistic; group belonging and social norms matter.
- Rice culture: if wheat pasta breaks the norm, wheat-based sales may suffer.
- Indulgence 38, low: may hint at reluctance toward new culinary experiences.
- Uncertainty avoidance 48: may affect trying a new food; needs a closer look.

**WHAT MATTERS FOR STAFF [1]**

- Power distance 78: Southeast Asian cultures expect more inequality between superiors and staff.
- Employees will not question orders or superiors: key for structuring a branch.

**SET ASIDE BY THE RESEARCH [1]**

- Motivation (46): not relevant; drives neither customer decisions nor the staff profile.
- Long-term orientation 29, relatively low: no relevance yet (6 Oct 2026); maybe operations later.

**NOT YET KNOWN [9]**

- No Indonesian evidence yet on collectivism or how wheat pasta is seen (Paul's task).
- Whether the target group differs from the general population, for example on indulgence.

### What it means for CORSA

- The rice norm is the biggest cultural risk to CORSA's core product.
- Hierarchy shapes training and store roles.
- Groups can be a sales channel (Jakarta customer brief, culture page).

### How to continue

- a Find Indonesian data on wheat versus rice meals (Paul's open task).
- b FIELD WEEK, IF CONFIRMED: Add a rice or grain base to the taste test; date open.
- c Map each relevant dimension to a CAGE (cultural, administrative, geographic, economic distance) question.

Note. Scores are national averages from The Culture Factor tool, not Jakarta customers; the Philippines and Switzerland are for comparison only.

**WAGES AND SPENDING [5]**

- Private consumption: 2.7 pp of 2025 growth, down from 2.8 pp in 2024.
- Insecurity is particularly pronounced among self-classified middle-class households.

**DIGITAL [5]**
