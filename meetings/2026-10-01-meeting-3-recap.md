## 02 · Recap

### Main topics

#### Team Roles and Coordination

27 min · Samuel, Tiago and Ana

The group worked through roles and titles based on members’ different strengths. Samuel discussed Marius’s financial work, Ana’s research and benchmarking, and Tiago’s marketing and market-analysis experience; Matthieu described a connecting role between finance and strategic fit, while Paul suggested “strategic advisor.” Tiago argued that Jonas’s realism and ability to keep work focused could be underused in a data-analyst-only role, and Samuel suggested Jonas provide Paul with an initial plan to challenge. The assignments and titles were still being worked through during the meeting.

Ana proposed dividing research by focus, with her concentrating on benchmarking and other work covering qualitative, cultural and geographical angles. Samuel supported differentiated ownership, asking people to validate their own work, share it with the team and seek help from colleagues whose skills fit the task. Speaker 1 had earlier raised how Team A’s cultural and segment findings could inform Team B’s economic location choice without creating conflicting conclusions or excessive exchanges. Ana later said that task division had not made it clear what others were researching or how the work would fit together.

OUTCOME: The WhatsApp structure and shared Drive approach were agreed, and Samuel’s recap listed the role allocation, but the detailed cross-team coordination and integration process remained open. (11:10, 30:58, 1:29:20)

#### Financial Model and Margins

24 min · Samuel, Marius and Paul

Marius said he would need suitable software and baseline research for the financial work, and suggested using a comparable business case because he did not yet have baseline figures such as an expected margin. Tiago emphasized checking numbers for reliability. Samuel identified the Jakarta business-case outputs as funding needs, CAPEX, OPEX, revenue forecast, scenarios and sensitivity analysis. The group discussed the inputs and outputs, but did not settle a detailed workflow or identify who would provide the comparable baseline.

The discussion of restaurant economics exposed unresolved assumptions. Marius estimated restaurant margins in Zurich at 2–3% and said rent and employees together might be 95% of costs, while Samuel estimated those costs could be 50% or 60%; the figures were not reconciled. Samuel asked the group to determine the highest possible margin within the next 3 weeks and develop its own answer rather than rely too heavily on comparisons or simply fill in a spreadsheet. He said Corsa would provide templates and structure, with deliverables to be reviewed and potentially divided after the one week of finding and defining.

Paul said the model should connect Group A’s findings with the Finnish market and be useful for presentation visuals. Marius proposed a standard business-case dashboard based on explicit assumptions, and Paul suggested including cash flow, CAPEX and OPEX, revenue and pricing assumptions, and cost control, with an illustrative price adjusted using willingness-to-pay research. Samuel said the main location should be modelled from at least 40 square meters, including kitchen and delivery access, and corrected the staffing recollection to 1 cook and 1 barista, with the barista also handling the cashier. He said the model should be conservative and explainable without claiming false precision, and usable for a possible investor.

OUTCOME: Marius was assigned the business-case dashboard and financial model, but the margin target, cost breakdown, baseline data and detailed model assumptions remained unresolved. (21:24, 48:39, 1:16:02)

#### Market Strategy and Franchise Direction

16 min · Samuel, Tiago and Ana

Tiago asked whether Samuel had documented market research, and Samuel said he had researched in his own way and had a Corsa GPT with highlighted chats and material he could provide. Tiago said the group could build on and challenge that work, while Ana asked whether the team was seeking new findings or merely reproducing Samuel’s results. Tiago also asked what Samuel ideally wanted Corsa to become in Indonesia in 2 years, including whether it would operate only there or elsewhere and whether it would be a pop-up or a long-term restaurant; that question was not answered in this part.

Samuel argued that franchising was necessary for Corsa to scale, referring to a 100 million valuation requiring 340 locations across Asia and separately to roughly 350 franchise models globally; the assumptions were questioned and not validated. Tiago argued that a system could work without being a franchise and that rapid high-scale development would be difficult. He asked whether the immediate goal was an Indonesia restaurant followed by franchising, and whether the first shop should impress prospective customers and Asian businesspeople who might replicate it. Samuel said it was too early to focus on franchising and the group should follow the current scope. For Group A’s research, Samuel asked the team to assess strategic and cultural fit, cited Oakberry as a benchmark, and suggested comparing Indonesia or the Philippines with Singapore and Dubai rather than copying other companies.

OUTCOME: The group did not settle Corsa’s future direction or franchise sequence; Samuel asked the team to focus on the current scope, while the research question of how to build on existing work and assess strategic and cultural fit remained open. (41:43, 44:30, 1:10:18)
