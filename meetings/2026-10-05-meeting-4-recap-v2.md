#### Customer Findings and Forecasts

12 min · Tiago, Samuel and Jonas

Tiago shared preliminary customer-profile research drawing on Oakberry and Starbucks. He reported that 56% valued healthfulness, customers liked Oakberry’s smaller portions and 80% liked customisation; he proposed different sizes and cited 78% of Gen Z and 67% of Millennials as liking meal-based subscriptions. He also suggested that customers may value a place to hang out and a sense of belonging. Samuel asked him to structure the findings, put the most important points first and use both text and graphics. The proposals remained for further research rather than being adopted.

Samuel said Oakberry should remain a close comparison, while Starbucks examples should be included when they have substantial effects on cash flow or community. He described Starbucks locations that were too large for students staying for hours and its voucher system, which he said generated cash flow. This gave the group a high-impact focus for comparisons, but did not settle a complete competitor scope. Tiago’s suggestions about portion sizes, subscriptions and a welcoming venue were recorded as research ideas, not confirmed product or business decisions.

Jonas asked whether the customer findings were relevant to forecasts, noting high customer acquisition costs in the first weeks and months of opening in Indonesia. Samuel proposed assessing impact by money affected and number of people affected; Jonas added lasting reach, whether people retain the name and whether they could become customers. Samuel cautioned that community benefits and viral attention may be difficult to measure or link to specific spending, but said they should still be listed. The discussion did not settle a final method for quantifying these effects.

**OUTCOME** The findings and high-impact comparisons are to be structured for further research, but the group did not decide which to include in forecasts or how to quantify community impact. (03:05, 08:56, 11:13)

#### Zurich Hybrid Location

7 min · Samuel, Matthieu and Tiago

Samuel said he and Joshua were aligned on finding a new location in Zurich, partly for cash-flow reasons, alongside the Indonesia focus. He described a possible pop-up that would be presented as a location for at least 1 year, and said a site could be less than 6 square meters, while 30 to 100 square meters would be welcome. He called the format hybrid, combining robotics and people rather than being fully autonomous or run only by humans. Corsa was also working with people at ETH and hoped to establish an official connection.

Matthieu suggested adapting a nearby pizza vending-machine concept to sell pasta. Samuel said Corsa’s fresh-supply and quality approach would need 5 to 7 square meters at minimum, compared with about 1 square meter for the pizza machines. Tiago argued that seeing a robot make pasta could be more impressive than receiving pasta from a machine, while Samuel resisted treating live cooking as a selling point. He said the first location’s approach was not yet known and emphasized execution and moving quickly so choices could be revisited as circumstances changed.

**OUTCOME** Finding a Zurich hybrid location remained a direction rather than a group decision, and the site size and whether customers would see the robot cooking remain open. (49:16, 52:44, 55:03)
