#### Customer Research and Forecasts

15 min · Tiago, Samuel and Jonas

Tiago presented customer research comparing Oakberry and Starbucks. He reported that 56% valued healthfulness, 80% of Oakberry customers liked customizability, and 78% of Gen Z and 67% of millennials liked meal-based subscriptions. He also described interest in both smaller and larger portions, and said customers may value a place to hang out and a sense of belonging as well as the food. Tiago committed to structuring the findings in a report, while Samuel asked for categorized, prioritized material presented with both text and graphics.

Samuel advised keeping Oakberry in view and comparing competitors on features with substantial effects on cash flow or community, rather than minor marketing details. He cautioned that Starbucks had bet on location and size, with many locations closing because they were too large for students staying for hours, and cited vouchers as a cash-flow-related feature. Jonas asked how findings should inform forecasts, noting high customer-acquisition costs in the first weeks and months of opening in Indonesia. Samuel proposed assessing money and people affected; Jonas added lasting reach and likelihood of retention, while Samuel said community effects should be listed even if initially hard to measure.

Jonas was researching restaurant-specific cost of goods sold and margins in relation to a targeted ROI, but said his figures were still broad and that he expected more reliable figures in a couple of days. Samuel noted that menu, operating data, staffing, margins and equipment research were not yet visible in the material he had seen, while accepting that the work could take more time. Later, Tiago offered a roster to help the team enter figures in the financial model and test whether assumptions made sense; Jonas described this as trial and error. No precise method for measuring impact or completed cost figures were established.

**OUTCOME** Tiago will structure the customer findings and Jonas will continue the cost and margin research, while the forecasting measures and final comparison scope remain unsettled. (03:05, 11:13, 14:09)

#### Indonesian Customer Research

15 min · Samuel, Tiago and Paul

Paul explained Hofstede's cultural-dimensions framework, saying Indonesia is highly collectivist, with group belonging, social norms and family highly important, and that power distance could matter for operations. Samuel saw collectivism as a possible opportunity for Corsa and asked the group to consider how it could apply. Samuel said not to share project documents with outside contacts, while authorizing Tiago to send the adjusted project-scope document with the red title page to the university. #### Zurich Hybrid Location

8 min · Samuel, Tiago and Paul

Samuel said he and Joshua were aligned on finding a new Zurich location for cash-flow reasons, describing a pop-up in practice that would not be marketed as a pop-up and would run for at least one year. He described a hybrid site using robotics and people, not a fully autonomous or human-run location, and said the team was working with ETH and hoped for an official connection. The size remained unclear: Samuel said it could be less than six square meters and that 30 to 40 to 50 to 100 square meters would be welcome, while later saying five to seven square meters was the minimum for fresh food and supply.

Paul suggested adapting an electric-vehicle charging-station pizza vending machine concept to sell pasta. Samuel said Corsa would need a higher-quality approach and that fresh food and supply made a very small machine unsuitable, citing five to seven square meters as a minimum compared with vending machines around one square meter and two meters high. Jonas offered a robotics club connection at ATA, but Samuel said Corsa was already pursuing students and professors and that support could be requested if needed. Tiago argued that customers seeing a robot make pasta would be more compelling than pressing a button; Samuel said the first hybrid location in Jakarta had not settled whether to show the kitchen, and that product experience and execution mattered more than live cooking.

**OUTCOME** The hybrid concept remains under consideration, but the site, size and whether customers will see the kitchen or robot remain unresolved; neither the vending-machine idea nor the robotics-club connection was adopted. (50:50, 52:44, 55:03)
