# PESTEL Jakarta: how is it there? (Pillar 1, draft for Team A)

Date: 2026-10-17. Status: DRAFT; Team A decides the wording. Not legal advice. Plan: `tasks/2026-10-17-run4-plan-pillar1.md` (W-PEST). Unverified.

**How to read.** PESTEL says *how is it there* (Indonesia and Jakarta, as sourced facts). CAGE (`reports/2026-10-17-cage-switzerland-jakarta.md`, being written) says *what the Switzerland-to-Jakarta differences mean for CORSA*; this report does not repeat that analysis and points to it where a topic belongs there. Every fact carries a tag `[row; geo-agefit]`. Geography: J Jakarta (DKI), U urban Indonesia, N national, P proxy (other country or non-representative sample). Age-fit: A exact, B/C partial, D no age split against the canonical bands (Gen Z 18-28, Younger Millennials 29-35, Older Millennials 36-45, Gen X 46-61). Only VERIFIED-OPENED or CORRECTED-OPENED rows are used; "relay" rows are tier-3 copies of an unopened original. Text after "Analysis:" or in the implication column is our reading, not sourced data. Confidence is ours: high = original opened, in force or measured; medium = relay or single source; low = proxy, small sample or gap. Row ids are listed in the Appendix with register id, period and verification. `DISC-A#` = row # of table A in `data/raw/2026-10-17-disconfirming-evidence.md`; `ENV-###` rows are in `data/processed/2026-10-pestel-environmental.csv`.

**Evidence limits, plainly.** No cited row gives a 25-39 split for Jakarta. Most rows are national (N) with no age split (D). Jakarta-specific rows are: population by age in two city administrations, per-capita spend, confidence index, wages, UMP, PBJT, flooding, and padel venue counts. Where a figure is national we say so.

## Summary for investors

- **Political:** the rules for a foreign-owned restaurant are open on paper (no foreign-ownership cap found for restaurants), but a successor investment-list regulation is in drafting and halal special provisions were announced on 9 Oct 2026. Realised investment grew in 2025 and H1 2026; Jakarta is the largest province by realised investment. Street protests in Jakarta did not target outlets in the cited reports.
- **Economic:** Jakarta spends more per head than the country and confidence is high, yet the national middle class and middle-class wages are shrinking and the top spending class saves more. Evidence supports an affluent-tier story, not a broad middle-class one.
- **Social:** price and taste lead stated choice; friends and family lead discovery; health interest is high but willingness to pay a 20% premium is 19% (national, food products). Sport communities are growing (Jakarta padel stock is documented).
- **Technological:** QRIS payments and delivery apps are mass-market; creator links reach far more people than they convert.
- **Environmental:** recurring flooding, land subsidence in the north, and packaging rules are real operating factors. Several items (air quality, Jakarta heat, cup rules, business tariffs) are gaps.
- **Legal:** the largest finding is the PT PMA investment minimum of IDR 10bn excluding land and building, per city and per 2-digit KBLI 56 [E-04; N-D], far above a single-outlet budget. Franchise to a local partner avoids it but brings its own tests. Halal certification is already mandatory for large businesses. See the regulatory report for routes.
