**Three points for the decision (Analysis: our reading of the rows, not sourced data).**
1. **The entry route reduces administrative distance but does not remove it (gap-analysis point B8).** A licence or franchise to an Indonesian-owned operator is not expected to trigger the IDR 10bn plan threshold and moves the permit, halal and local-tax burden to the operator. It adds a franchise registration (STPW) on both sides, a profit test for the franchisor (3 years plus 2 audited profit years, or 5 years under an older rule), royalty withholding of 10% and a brand risk, because halal must be consistent in every outlet. Section 3.4.
2. **Culture is a smaller barrier than the national indices suggest, and a larger one than the café evidence suggests.** Hofstede scores are national averages and cannot predict who will buy a pasta bowl; the observed Jakarta habits (coffee for gathering 44%, run clubs ending at cafés) support the format. The open risks are the rice habit (62% eat rice daily in a national online panel [CAGE-020; N-D] (high)), halal trust, and how the Italian racing identity is read. Section 4.
3. **Geography is far but not decisive.** The decisions sit in the catchment: which of SCBD, Senopati and Kuningan has enough lunch and after-work guests, and flood and access exposure. Matthieu's area list is a hypothesis; no verified row yet counts customers in any of the five areas. Section 5.

**Time limits.** SEHATI (the free halal scheme) ran in 2025 only. T-06 (20% PBJT reduction, Aug-Dec 2025) expired 31 Jan 2026 and T-07 (March 2026 only) has expired; do not budget either. H-04 (BPJPH: no postponement of 18 Oct 2026; special provisions in drafting) and H-12 (Minister's decision on imported products) must be re-checked on 19-20 Oct 2026 because the micro and small business phase starts on 18 Oct 2026.

**Matthieu's claims (Appendix A).** 174 claims registered: 161 verified, 5 corrected, 8 unverified (section A.1). Where a claim stays unverified the table says why and what Matthieu intended.

## 2. Method and limits

- **Source rule.** Only rows marked VERIFIED-OPENED or CORRECTED-OPENED are used. BLOCKED rows are excluded and named in section 8. Relay rows (news or data portals quoting an unopened original) are tier 3 and are marked "relay" in the row, not here.
- **National or proxy evidence.** Hofstede scores are national; the Jakpat coffee and rice surveys are national online panels; the Jakpat local food survey covers eight Java cities (Bogor, Depok, Bandung, Yogyakarta, Surakarta, Semarang, Malang, Surabaya) with no Jakarta in the sample; NIQ is an online panel of adults in 19 countries; legal rows are national, with PBJT (Jakarta tax) as the exception. Say so wherever a number is used.
- **New evidence.** To fill the cultural and geographic gaps the worker registered 31 sources in this run (REG-100 to REG-130) and opened 27 of them; four (REG-127 to REG-130) could not be opened and wrote 40 rows to `data/processed/2026-10-cage-geo-culture.csv` (30 verified rows, 10 gap rows; CAGE-014, CAGE-019 and CAGE-025 are pre-window historical context and not counted as evidence of today's market).
- **What CAGE cannot cover (gap-analysis B8).** CAGE measures distance from the home country and is risk-centred; it cannot by itself test demand, competition or unit economics. Those sit in Plan 1 (demand), Plan 3 (competitors and prices) and the financial model; this report only maps what they show onto the four distances. The framework was chosen partly for presentation fit (M7), so it is applied after the fact to briefs that were not structured by CAGE.
- **Not done.** No Swiss-side comparison data; no BMKG station normals (the WMO table hosted by the Japan Meteorological Agency is used instead, normals period not printed); no tier 1 source for flight distance. These are listed in Appendix C.

Matthieu's administrative papers (12 and 15 Oct) focus on halal; Plan 2 (`reports/2026-10-17-regulatory-entry-pathway.md`) covers the full pathway.

### 3.3 What it means for CORSA
**Distance.** Large on three points that have no Swiss counterpart in the rows: a minimum investment plan for foreign-owned companies, a mandatory religious-product certification that reaches the kitchen layout, and a franchise regime with a profit test. Switzerland has no equivalent halal duty; Swiss law was not compared (Matthieu says the same).

**Transfers well.** Restaurants are open to foreign investors and the permit route is a standard business-number plus standard-certificate route. [E-09; N-na] (high) Taxes are published and stable on paper (PBJT 10%, corporate tax 22%). [T-08; N-na] (high)

**Needs adapting.** The operating model: a halal-compliant kitchen with dedicated storage and separated serving (Matthieu's "do not transfer the Swiss operating model"); documentation of every imported input; for packaged foreign products the foreign certificate must be registered and recognised, and whether this reaches restaurant ingredients is open [CAGE-024; N-na] (medium: whether a Swiss certifier is recognised was not checked); the investment frame.

**Risk to test.** (1) Whether a PMA counts as "large" for halal (assumed in Plan 2; no text states it). [H-03; N-na] (medium) (2) Whether the halal "corner" in a mixed kitchen can be certified (Plan 2: not supported by the text; open with BPJPH). [H-06; N-na] (medium) (3) The franchise profit test for a young brand. [F-04; N-na] (high). (4) Policy flux: a new investment Perpres is in a one-year drafting programme. [E-02; N-na] (medium, relay)

Plan 2's recommendation to test the licence route first rests on text and is not legally cleared (lawyer questions 3, 6-9).

### 6.3 What it means for CORSA
