**How to read the figures.** Each figure is followed by a tag `[row id; geography-agefit]`. Row ids point to `data/processed/plan4-*.csv` and `demand/*.csv`; register id, reference period and base of every row are in the Appendix. Geography: J Jakarta, J* Jabodetabek or Greater Jakarta scope (mall survey, venue count; caveat in the text), U urban Indonesia (mostly Java; the Jakpat September 2025 sample excludes Jakarta), N national, P proxy (other markets or categories). ## Summary

- **Evidence base.** No row gives a 25-39 cut. Of 137 distinct rows cited, 12 Jakarta (J), 3 Jakarta-coded with a caveat (J*), 22 urban Java (U), 97 national (N), 3 proxy (P). Findings are directional, not Jakarta estimates.
- **Verdicts** (scale: supported / partly supported / not supported / insufficient evidence). H1 insufficient evidence, nominally against (one survey, 44% against 40%, inside the margin of error). H2 insufficient evidence. H3 partly supported at the stated level, purchases untested (one vendor release, food products). H4 insufficient evidence (scale documented; one platform shows 58% aged 21-40, no baseline; purchase influence absent). H5 insufficient evidence. H6 insufficient evidence (attendance only). H7 insufficient evidence. H8 insufficient evidence.
- **Discovery.** Friends and family come first, 44% [P4W1-R04; U-D], then TikTok food influencer accounts 40% [P4W1-R05; U-D]. Margin of error under five points; no age split or Jakarta cut.
- **Choice and health.** Price attracts 64% to a new place [UA-N58; U-D]; tasty menu is the top revisit reason, 83% [P4W7b-14; N-D]. For 65% healthy nutrition matters more than five years ago [P4W4-04; N-D]; 48% would pay more than 10% extra for food with added nutrition [P4W4-01; N-D], 19% would pay 20% extra [P4W4-02; N-D] (food products, not restaurants).
- **Identity transfer.** Racing, Italian origin and functional drinks have no audience or association data; the racing rows are attendance counts. They stay hypotheses.

## Findings by trigger

### Discovery: friends, social platforms, creators, reviews (H1)

- First learned of a new eating place (Jakpat, eight Java cities without Jakarta, multiple answers, do not sum): friends and family 44% [P4W1-R04; U-D]; TikTok food influencer accounts 40% [P4W1-R05; U-D]; social media advertising 39% [P4W1-R06; U-D]; passing by 35% [P4W1-R07; U-D]. This is the only comparison of first-learned-of routes.
- Reasons to try a new place (same survey, subset, base conflict unresolved in the row): social media or ads 44% [P4W2b-03; U-D]; good reviews or ratings 42% [P4W2b-02; U-D].
- Trust in decision sources (influence score out of 4, six SEA markets, not food-specific): family and friends 2.42, online reviews 2.36, creators 1.98 [P4W2b-11; P-D]. Bought because a creator recommended it: 67% of consumers, six markets [P4W2b-12; P-D]. The Indonesian headline "shop through creator-shared links", 76% [P4W2-01; N-D], comes from a mixed sample of consumers, creators and experts. The relay's headline mentions declining trust; no trend figure is stored.
- Context on conversion (used in no verdict or entry change): opened an affiliate link 83% against bought through one in six months, under 40% [P4W2b-14; N-D]; routinely watch live shopping 92% [P4W2b-15; N-C]; ever bought in a live session 46% (1H 2024) and 44% (2H 2025) [P4W2b-16; N-D]. Not food-specific.
- Qualitative: trust in food-vlogger reviews fell after a scandal (n=85) [P4W2b-21; N-C]; micro and nano influencers hold trust better, yet mega influencers still lead in Indonesia [P4W2b-06; P-D]; Google Maps ratings raised trust at two Surakarta restaurants (non-probability, n=400) [P4W2b-19; U-D].

Analysis: Friends, TikTok food accounts and social ads sit close together, none with an age cut. The rows show neither that social platforms lead nor that they do not matter, and say nothing about creator return on spend.

### Novelty and trend (H2)

- YouGov adults, no age split: self-identified early adopters 7% [P4W3-04; N-D]; need-based shoppers 52% [P4W3-08; N-D]. Among "pulse chasers", decreased spending 39% [UB-07; N-D] and increased spending 23% [UB-08; N-D].
- Populix panel (Millennials and Gen Z): men who try every new food trend 14% [P4W3-01; N-D]; women attracted by a social media trend, about 30% [P4W3-03; N-D]; Millennials described as more inclined than Gen Z to try trends (qualitative) [UB-02; N-D].
- Would try a new place 73% [P4W2b-04; U-D]; unique menu attracts 49% [UA-N59; U-D]; opening promotions attract 48% [UA-N60; U-D]. In the timing question, 26% say they visit because of opening promos [UA-N63; U-D]: a different question of the same survey, so the two figures do not conflict.
- Timing: by chance or one to two weeks after opening 35% [UA-N62; U-D]; during the grand opening 16% [UA-N64; U-D]; wait until stable or reviews are seen 40% [UA-N61; U-D].
- A Jakarta Gen Z viral-marketing study has only its sample size in a verified row, 209 respondents [P4W3-09; J-B]; its effect size is not used.

Analysis: Stated willingness to try is high, but self-described early adopters are few and need-based shoppers a majority. Few come during the grand opening and many wait for reviews. No row measures repeat visits after a novelty-led first visit, so "trial only" cannot be tested (a gap). The one generation comparison runs against "Gen Z leads".

### Health (H3)

- Proactive steps for health 46% [P4W4-03; N-D]. Barriers: healthy options hard to find 48% [P4W4-05; N-D]; high cost 43% [P4W4-06; N-D]; doubts that health products work 43% [P4W4-07; N-D]. A medical specialist's recommendation is the top influence on wellness purchases 77% [P4W4-08; N-D].

Analysis: The NIQ figures are stated, national, without age split, from one vendor release (Indonesia n not on the page), and cover food and wellness products, not restaurants. Nearly half accepting a 10% premium is not "modest" by itself; the drop to 19% at 20% is what caps it. Nothing here shows what people order.

### Price, promotions, taste and delivery

- Choosing where to eat: price 60% [UA-N65; U-D]; taste 59% [P4W7b-02; U-D]; service 44%, cleanliness 44%, portion size 43% [P4W7b-03; U-D]. Gen Z coffee consumers: price is the main factor 47% [UE-N03; N-B]; taste 46% in the same row.
- Promotions in delivery apps (Jakpat, national): check discounts before ordering 67% [UA-N54; N-D]; promo as main reason to use apps 55% [SD01; N-D].
- Most used delivery app: GoFood 38% [P4W1-R08; N-D]; by generation Gen Z ShopeeFood 39% [P4W1-R11; N-C], Millennials GoFood 37% [P4W1-R10; N-C], Gen X GoFood 43% [P4W1-R09; N-C].
- Delivery GMV 2025 (Momentum Works estimates relayed by Databoks, include cancelled orders, USD): the three apps together +18% [P4W10-22; N-D]; GrabFood USD 2.94 bn, +16% [P4W10-23; N-D]; GoFood USD 1.98 bn, +5% [P4W10-24; N-D]; ShopeeFood USD 1.47 bn, +51% [P4W10-25; N-D]. The page calls GoFood likely to stagnate.
- Order value (Snapcart, typical spend per order, Jan 2025): under IDR 50,000 42% [SD05; N-D]; IDR 50,000-100,000 42% [SD06; N-D]; IDR 100,000-150,000 11% [UD-N05; N-D]. Average lunch order via apps (Jakpat, Mar 2025): Gen Z IDR 58,609 [P4W9-06; N-B]; Millennials IDR 65,130 [P4W9-05; N-C]; Gen X IDR 63,250 [P4W9-07; N-C].
- **Plan 3 cross-reference (listed menu prices in `price-ladder.csv`; statistics in `data/raw/2026-10-17-price-points.md` section 6).** Coffee median IDR 29,000 (23 items, 6 brands, 10 approximate prices, 61% Fore Coffee); bowl median IDR 60,000 (15 items, 2 brands, 80% Burgreens, whose list prices exclude 10% tax and service). Limits: few brands, mixed price bases, not market medians (the bowl cell has under four brands). For comparison, Gen Z coffee consumers spending under IDR 10,000 per cup 48% [P1C3-01; N-B] and IDR 10,000-25,000 per cup 30% [UE-N04; N-B] (the item includes coffee made at home).

Analysis: Price and taste are the most consistent stated drivers and tie within the surveys' margin of error. Promotions are heavily used in delivery, but the rows do not show what a promotion does to spend per order. Delivery GMV growth differs sharply by app (ShopeeFood fastest from the smallest base, GoFood slowest), which matters for the first listing; growth rates are not shares, and order value or frequency per app was not found. The listed coffee median lies above the two largest per-cup spend bands, and a bowl at the median is in the range of the average lunch order via apps; both are indications only: listed prices of a few brands against survey bases that differ (including home-made coffee).

### Community and sport (H4)

- Running: Strava running clubs in Indonesia about 6x the previous year ("hampir 6x lipat") [UC-N02; N-D]; runners who prefer to run with others 33% ("hampir 1 dari 3") [UC-N01; N-D]; Jakarta Running Festival 27,300 runners [P4W5-04; J-D], up 70% on 2024 [UC-N04; J-D].
- Padel stock, end-2025 (Core & Court count relayed by Databoks; original not opened): 326 courts in DKI Jakarta [P4W10-01; J-D] against 947 in Indonesia [P4W10-02; N-D]; 34 clubs in South Jakarta [P4W10-05; J-D], with 61 more under construction (area inferred from sentence order) [P4W10-06; J-D]. The 326 courts, the 397 reported by CNA in March 2026 (courts or buildings, unresolved) and 95 venues in Greater Jakarta (not DKI only) in mid-August 2025 [P4W10-08; J*-D] use different units and must not be compared.
- Age and sex (Liga.Tennis platform, about 100,000 users in seven clubs, all racquet sports; the article does not state club locations, so the rows are coded N): ages 21-40 account for 58.30% of padel participation [P4W10-10; N-C]; the largest user segment is 31-40, with 57.3% male and 42.7% female users [P4W10-12; N-C]. One operator's platform, not a market.
- Venue F&B: an average racquet-sport visit costs about IDR 400,000 for the court plus about IDR 50,000 for consumption [P4W10-11; N-D] (approximate, all racquet sports, not padel-only). One operator (WePadl, n=1) calls F&B essential to a padel venue (qualitative) [P4W10-09; J-D].
- Platform and permit context: Playtomic reports GMV per court per month up 173% in 2023-2024 (platform revenue, not participation) [P4W5-07; N-D] and "+50 clubs for 25M inhabitants" for Jakarta (ambiguous slide wording) [UC-N06; J-D]. 185 of 397 (about 46%) Jakarta padel sites lack a building permit [UC-N07; J-D].

Analysis: Event entrants, club counts and court revenue show scale, and one operator's platform has 58.30% of padel participation aged 21-40, without a population baseline. The only F&B evidence is on-site consumption at venues and one operator's view; no row shows that communities change where people buy elsewhere. Strava users are self-selected. Missing purchase-influence data is a gap, not evidence of no effect. The permit findings are an operating risk for padel venues as partners.

### Foreign-brand attitude and boycott (H5)

- Prefer local culinary businesses when eating out 77% [UA-N66; U-D]. The item concerns local cuisine and local businesses; it has no foreign-brand comparison.
- KFC operator (FAST), nine-month net loss narrowed from IDR 557.08 bn to IDR 239.58 bn (about 57% lower, per the row note) [Q6-001; N-D]. Management reported 19 outlets closed and about 400 layoffs at the October 2025 expose [Q6-004; N-D]; the later filing shows 20 outlets closed and 1,041 fewer employees [UC-N08; N-D], a different measure.
- Starbucks licensee (MAPB), nine-month net loss up from IDR 79.13 bn to IDR 108.69 bn [Q6-005; N-D]. Management says Starbucks was removed from the boycott list and sentiment is improving (company claim) [UC-N11; N-D].
- Pizza Hut operator (PZZA), FY2025 profit IDR 24.75 bn against a FY2024 loss of IDR 72.83 bn [Q6-007; N-D]. Attribution to boycott fade or efficiency was not verified (row note).

Analysis: Results are mixed (one loss narrowed, one widened, one return to profit) and no opened source attributes them to the boycott. They are company results of US-linked chains, not consumer-attitude data, and say nothing about a Swiss-Italian start-up. No country-of-origin study and no Italian-specific row was found among the opened sources.

### Racing and Italian origin (H6, Q8)

- Mandalika MotoGP spectators: 102,929 (2023) and 121,252 (2024) [Q8-003; N-D]; 140,324 (3-5 Oct 2025) [Q8-001; N-D], up 15.7% on 2024 [Q8-004; N-D]; 157,875 (9-11 Oct 2026) [P4W10-28; N-D]. Counts only: no age, sex or origin split, and the circuit is in Lombok, not Jakarta.
- Documented gap, racing (`data/raw/plan4-misc-run3/findings.md`, GAP-Q8-1): no Dorna, MGPA or ITDC audience profile, no Indonesian split of the F1 audience and no Ducati or Ferrari perception study were found. The Antara article recommends a post-event survey.
- Documented gap, Italian origin (GAP-Q8-2): the tier-1 Italian trade file shows Indonesia only as a source of Italy's food imports; no Italy-to-Indonesia food export value, Italian-restaurant demand figure or Italian image study for Indonesia was opened. Italian trade-body hosts were unreachable.

Analysis: Attendance is growing, but who attends is unknown; both identity layers are untested for the target segments.

### Third place, venue attributes and functional drinks (H7)

- Coffee as a place to gather with friends 44% [UC-N13; N-D]; to focus on work or study 34% [P4W7b-01; N-D]; leisure time in cafes as self-reward 49.1% [P4W7b-13; N-D].
- Reasons for revisiting the same cafe (survey run by the cafe outlet Kedai Kopi, middle-class respondents, sampling not stated, relayed by GoodStats): tasty menu 83%, comfortable room 78%, good service 70%, clean room 64%, good facilities 45%, aesthetics 27% [P4W7b-14; N-D]. Aesthetics ranks last of six prompted items; comfort, cleanliness and facilities are atmosphere-adjacent and rank in between.
- Mall visitors naming dining as the most common activity 68% [P1C3-02; J*-D]; invitations from friends and family as the main external factor in mall visits 65% [UE-N06; J*-D]. J*: Jabodetabek upscale and mid-range malls, 71% male, non-probability, n=266 of 800.
- Jakarta Gen Z, five coffee-shop brands: servicescape effect on satisfaction not significant, service quality and food and beverage quality significant; satisfaction and brand attitude both drive revisit intention [P4W7b-15; J-B].
- Functional drinks: no uptake series; matcha (a trend-drink proxy, see age section) is not one. Clearer product labels wanted 62% [UB-14; N-D], a transparency demand rather than scepticism.

Analysis: Gathering, working and self-reward motives are documented in national samples; dwell time is not. In the revisit ranking, menu and comfort sit above aesthetics, and the Jakarta Gen Z result points the same way, though brand attitude matters for return.

## Hypothesis tests

Each verdict is tested against the plan's own disconfirmers (plan 4, section 2). Confirmation needs evidence for the 25-39 bands, which no row provides.

**H1 Social discovery is the main first-visit trigger.** The plan's four disconfirmers: (1) friends or word of mouth lead: met nominally, 44% against 40% [P4W1-R04, P4W1-R05; U-D], inside the margin of error and from one survey; (2) promos lead: price 64% and opening promotions 48% rank above social media or ads 44% as stated trial attractors [UA-N58, UA-N60, P4W2b-03; U-D], but this is a different question from first-learned-of; (3) 29+ cohorts rely on Maps or apps: not tested, no row; (4) creator trust falling: one qualitative case (n=85) and a regional ranking with creators last, no trend. **Verdict: insufficient evidence, nominally against.** Entry change (Analysis): test peer referral (bring-a-friend), Maps and review presence and delivery-app promotions alongside creators; do not move budget on this evidence alone.

**H2 Novelty and viral appeal.** The weak-repeat disconfirmer is untestable (no repeat row); one qualitative row runs against "especially Gen Z". **Verdict: insufficient evidence.** Entry change: build the opening on taste and a repeatable routine (lunch, coffee) and measure repeat early.

**H3 Health motive is real but price-capped.** Stated importance 65%, a premium over 10% accepted by 48%, 20% by 19% (rows above). Observed purchases are untested; the disconfirmer (health as a top-three driver with observed uplift in Jakarta) is not met, which is an absence of rows, not proof. **Verdict: partly supported at the stated level; purchases untested; low confidence.** Entry change: lead with taste, convenience and "balanced"; keep health a secondary claim within a modest premium.

**H4 Run clubs and padel are an acquisition channel.** Partly met: large and growing participation, a 21-40 share on one platform (no baseline). Unmet: clubs meeting at cafes, influence on venue or product choice. **Verdict: insufficient evidence.** Entry change: treat community as a brand-building test (two tracked events, organiser interviews), treat padel-venue cafes as a hypothesis for the organiser interviews (the evidence is one operator quote), and keep delivery and office lunch as the base case until club-level conversion is measured.

**H5 Italian or European origin is neutral to mildly positive.** Chain results are mixed and unattributed; the local-preference figure is a local-cuisine item. No origin evidence exists, so no disconfirmer is shown or ruled out. **Verdict: insufficient evidence.** Entry change: lead with product and local partnerships, not "foreign import"; test spontaneous associations and perceived foreign ownership.

**H6 Racing identity resonates.** Attendance is growing; no audience profile exists. **Verdict: insufficient evidence.** Entry change: racing as a secondary design and story layer until a concept-card test reads it.

**H7 Functional drinks, community and third place transfer.** Third-place gathering, work and self-reward motives are documented; dwell time is not. No series shows functional-drink uptake rising. **Verdict: insufficient evidence** (third place partly documented, functional drinks not). Entry change: drinks in a supporting role; test the menu before any drinks-led concept.

**H8 Halal cues influence venue choice.** Consumers who buy depending on a halal label 83% [P4W7b-18; N-D]: a 2023 vendor survey cited by the halal-assurance agency, all product categories, headline ("aware") in conflict with the quote ("buy"); no venue, age or Jakarta row. **Verdict: insufficient evidence.** Entry change: certification stays an entry condition (plan 2); do not size the marketing weight of halal before a primary survey item runs.

**Assumptions most at risk (Analysis).** "Social first", "premium without a price barrier" and "atmosphere and novelty bring people back" are untested or weakly supported: reasons to test, not proofs of failure. Facts pointing the other way (national unless marked; none has a Jakarta-by-age cut):

- Middle-class budgets: middle-class food consumption (Susenas household food group, not restaurant spend) grew 0.9% in 2025 against 4.6% in 2024, while non-food grew 6.4% [P1C2-09; N-D]; the share of workers earning a middle-class income fell from 14.5% (2018) to 7.1% (2025) [P1C2-10; N-D]; median real wages in medium- and high-skilled jobs fell by about 1% and 2% a year over 2018-2025 (the row note links the high-skilled group to office workers) [P1C2-11; N-D]; the Mandiri Saving Index for the middle savings group was 100.4 in February 2026, the lowest since October 2023 [P1C2-33; N-D].
- Eating out as a cut item: dining out (23%) is among the top categories Gen X+ respondents reduced [P4W9-28; N-D]; Millennials name takeout and international travel as their main areas of retrenchment (qualitative; single relay, and Detik names savings and holidays instead) [P4W9-29; N-D]; 66% of Millennials name cooking at home as a money-saving strategy (pre-window fieldwork) [P4W9-16; U-C].
- A premium foreign comparable: nine-month 2025 sales of MAPB, the Starbucks Indonesia licensee, fell 2.77% (IDR 2.42 trillion to 2.35 trillion) while its loss widened [Q6-006, Q6-005; N-D].
- Mixed signals: Jakarta consumer confidence index 146.8 in Sep 2026, 3.2 points below August (150.0) [P1C2-20; J-D]; restaurant spending in Mandiri card data grew 4.7% yoy in Q3 2026 against 6.1% in 2025 (period labels inferred), still the fastest-growing monitored category but slowing [P1C2-29; N-D].

The BLOCKED report naming eating out among the most-cut items (disconfirming-evidence table A, row 8) is excluded under the method rule. No national-culture argument is used; group effects appear only where a survey item measures them.

## Method and limits

Rows with verification VERIFIED-OPENED or CORRECTED-OPENED in `data/processed/plan4-*.csv` and, for spend context, `demand/*.csv`; no new research. Tiers: APJII, Playtomic and the journal articles are tier 2; the MAPB filing tier 1; the rest (Jakpat, Populix, YouGov, impact.com, NIQ, Strava, Core & Court, news relays) tier 3. No claim rests on tier 4. Many rows relay an unopened original; single-relay figures are weaker. Fieldwork before 2024-10-16 (IMGR Mar-Aug 2024, YouGov Sep 2024, halal 2023) is marked "pre-window" in the Appendix; it was published after the cutoff and is used as context only, which departs from the plan 8 date rule. Blocked rows are excluded. Payment (QRIS, PayLater) rows are in the matrix only. The README entry for the matrix and `plan4-misc-run3.csv` is still to be added.
