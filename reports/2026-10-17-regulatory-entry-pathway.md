# Regulatory and entry-route pathway: CORSA in Jakarta (Plan 2)

Date: 2026-10-17. Author: P2-A9 (synthesis). Status: draft for owner review. This is research, not legal advice (section 8).

**Reading guide.** Each legal statement ends with a tag `[row_id · register_id · instrument and article]`. The row is in `data/processed/2026-10-p2-*.csv`, `listed-operators.csv` or `plan4-origin-boycott.csv`, and has verification VERIFIED-OPENED or CORRECTED-OPENED. "Text:" or a plain sentence with a tag reports what the instrument says. Anything headed "Analysis:" is my interpretation and carries no authority. ## 1. Summary

**Deal-breakers (for the current frame of one outlet, about CHF 200k):**

1. **Investment threshold.** A PMA (foreign-owned PT) must show a planned investment above IDR 10,000,000,000, excluding land and building. For food and beverage services the base is the first 2 digits of the KBLI per "location point", and a location point is a kabupaten/kota, not an outlet. [E-04 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 26(2), 26(3)(b), 26(4)] PP 28/2025 repeats the amount and the 2-digit rule. [E-05 · REG-002 · PP 28/2025 Art. 212(2), 212(3)(b)] Failing the Art. 26 investment limit is also a ground for administrative sanction in supervision. [E-04 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 364(3)(h)] No single-outlet, SME or phasing relief was found. [E-04 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 26-27] Separately, a PT needs paid-up capital of at least IDR 2.5bn. [E-03 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 26(10)]
2. **Halal is already mandatory for a restaurant of medium or large size.** The medium and large phase ended on 17 Oct 2024 and the obligation applies from 18 Oct 2024. [H-02 · REG-009 · PP 42/2024 Art. 160(1)] A PMA is classed as a large business in the investment rules. [E-07 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 26(1)] Place and tools of the halal process must be separated from non-halal ones, including for serving. [H-06 · REG-009 · PP 42/2024 Art. 6(4), 20, 21, 22(3)] No text on certifying a "halal corner" inside a mixed kitchen was found. [H-06 · REG-009 · PP 42/2024 Art. 6(4)]
3. **Franchise-profit test.** A franchisor must show the franchised business ran at least 3 consecutive years plus 2 audited profitable years. [F-04 · REG-029 · PP 35/2024 Art. 4(5)] A young concept may fail this. Permendag 71/2019, still "Berlaku", says 5 years. [F-05 · REG-030 · Permendag 71/2019 Art. 2(3)]
4. **Watch item.** A new Perpres on investment business fields is in a one-year drafting programme from 22 Dec 2025; none was found. [E-02 · REG-007 · Keppres 38/2025 Diktum Kedua, via DDTC, tier 3]

**Not barriers on the text:** restaurants and cafes are open to foreign investors with no ownership cap found [E-01 · REG-003 · Perpres 10/2021 Art. 3(1)(d), 3(2)]; the permit is NIB plus Sertifikat Standar [E-09 · REG-002 · PP 28/2025 Art. 131, 132]; PBJT is 10% [T-01 · REG-019 · Perda DKI 1/2024 Art. 53(1)]; CIT is 22% [T-08 · REG-025 · UU PPh Art. 17(1)(b), as summarised by DJP].

Analysis: the route with the lowest legal hurdle on the text is a franchise or licence to an Indonesian-owned operator, because the PMA threshold does not attach to it. It depends on CORSA passing the PP 35/2024 profit test or on the licence falling outside "Waralaba". Both points need a lawyer (section 6). An own PT PMA outlet needs capital well above CHF 200k.

### 2.2 What the instruments say (shared by the routes)

**Investment and capital**
- Planned investment above IDR 10bn, per 2-digit KBLI per kabupaten/kota (above). Working capital for one turnover, including wages, rent and operating costs, is part of the plan that OSS validates for a PMA. [E-06 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 37(3)-(4)]
- Paid-up capital cannot leave the company account for at least 12 months, except for asset purchase, construction or operations. [E-03 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 27(1)]
- KBLI codes: 56101 (food provision in a fixed building) and 56303 (rumah minum/kafe). [E-12 · REG-005 · Peraturan BPS 7/2025]
- The 2-digit grouping covering both codes is my reading, not a listing in the text (Analysis, lawyer question 2).

**Licences**
- Medium-high risk needs NIB plus a verified Sertifikat Standar; an unverified certificate is only a basis for preparation. [E-09 · REG-002 · PP 28/2025 Art. 132(1)-(6)]
- Hukumonline (tier 3) puts KBLI 56101 at large scale (PT PMA) at medium-high risk, citing Lampiran I.L; the annex was not opened. [E-10 · REG-008 · PP 28/2025 Lampiran I.L, via Hukumonline]
- SLHS, a tourism certificate and RPTKA are tier-3 leads only. [E-11 · REG-008 · PP 28/2025 Art. 170(e)]
- In a mall or office building whose owner holds KKPR, PL, PBG and SLF, the tenant may use them; OSS verifies within 5 working days. No official total lead time or fee was found. [E-13 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 41-42]

**Halal**
- Food, drink and services of processing and serving must be certified. [H-01 · REG-009 · PP 42/2024 Art. 2(1), 155, 159(2)(a)]
- Self-declaration exists only for micro and small businesses with low-risk products. [H-03 · REG-009 · PP 42/2024 Art. 98(1)-(2)]
- Products made from forbidden ingredients are exempt but must carry a visible non-halal statement. [H-05 · REG-009 · PP 42/2024 Art. 2(2)-(3), 110(1)-(3)]
- The guideline covers restaurants including franchise restaurants and branches, with separation in every kitchen, warehouse, branch and outlet, an internal audit at least yearly and a report every 6 months. [H-07 · REG-018 · Kepkaban 78/2023 Bab II A.2(a), B.3(b), B.5]
- BPJPH tariff per certificate: IDR 12,500,000 for large and/or foreign-origin businesses, IDR 5,000,000 medium, IDR 300,000 micro/small. [H-08 · REG-017 · Kepkaban 22/2024 Lampiran II A.2]
- LPH fee: Lampiran IV row 17 (food and drink with processing) has a unit-cost ceiling of IDR 3,687,500 for medium, large and foreign; row 47 has IDR 5,275,000. Which row applies is for the LPH. [H-09 · REG-017 · Kepkaban 22/2024 Lampiran IV]
- Statutory maxima: payment 5, inspection 15 (+10), MUI decision 3, certificate 1 working days. [H-10 · REG-009 · PP 42/2024 Art. 79(4), 82, 86(3), 88(1)]
- Sanctions for breaching Art. 2(1): written warning and withdrawal from circulation; the fine ceiling of IDR 2bn does not attach to Art. 2(1). BPJPH itself says closure applies to medium and large restaurants. [H-11 · REG-009 · PP 42/2024 Art. 170(2), 170(7), 171(2)-(5)]
- BPJPH says mandatory halal continues on 18 Oct 2026 with no postponement; special provisions for UMK and imports are in drafting. [H-04 · REG-012 · BPJPH statement of 9 Oct 2026, not a regulation]
- For imports, the Minister must set the obligation by 17 Oct 2026; registration of a foreign certificate costs IDR 800,000. No Minister decision was found. [H-12 · REG-009 · PP 42/2024 Art. 160(3)] [H-12 · REG-017 · Kepkaban 22/2024 Lampiran II A.3]

**Tax**
- PBJT on food and drink in DKI is 10%. [T-01 · REG-019 · Perda DKI 1/2024 Art. 53(1)] A restaurant has at least tables, chairs and/or utensils. [T-02 · REG-019 · Perda DKI 1/2024 Art. 45(1)(a)] The base is the payment received. [T-03 · REG-019 · Perda DKI 1/2024 Art. 51(1)(a)]
- Sales are excluded when monthly turnover does not exceed IDR 42,000,000. [T-04 · REG-019 · Perda DKI 1/2024 Art. 45(2)(a)]
- Temporary 20% reductions were granted for Aug-Dec 2025 (expired 31 Jan 2026) and, per DDTC only, March 2026. [T-06 · REG-021 · Kepgub 722/2025 Diktum KESATU(b), KELIMA] [T-07 · REG-022 · Kepgub 310/2026, relay]
- CIT is 22% of taxable income; the DJP deck gives 11% only for turnover up to IDR 4.8bn. [T-08 · REG-025 · UU PPh Art. 17(1)(b), Art. 31E]
- Domestic withholding on payments to a foreign taxpayer is 20% without treaty relief. [T-09 · REG-026 · UU PPh Pasal 26(1)]
- Switzerland treaty ceilings: dividends 10% (company holding directly at least 25%) or 15% [T-10 · REG-024 · Treaty Art. 10(2)]; royalties 10% [T-11 · REG-041 · 2007 Protocol Art. 2]; service fees 5% [T-13 · REG-024 · Treaty Art. 13(2)]. The 1988 text alone says 12.5% for royalties; the later instrument prevails. [T-12 · REG-024 · Treaty Art. 12(2)]
- VAT: statutory 12%, effective 11% through a DPP of 11/12 (relay; PMK not opened). [T-15 · REG-028 · PMK 131/2024 Pasal 3, via DDTC]

**Franchise**
- PP 35/2024 revoked PP 42/2007. [F-01 · REG-029 · PP 35/2024 Art. 41]
- Every party needs an STPW, issued through OSS. [F-02 · REG-029 · PP 35/2024 Art. 12, 13(1), 14(1), 15(1)]
- A foreign franchisor adds a legalised home-country licence and a continuity statement from the Indonesian trade attache. The franchisee of a foreign franchise attaches the franchisor's STPW and receives its own centrally. [F-03 · REG-029 · PP 35/2024 Art. 13(3), 14(3), 15(2)-(3)]
- Regional STPW (domestic-franchise franchisees) takes at most 5 working days; this does not amend the 3/2-year test. [F-09 · REG-031 · Permendag 25/2025 Art. 4(11)-(12)]
- Prospectus at least 2 weeks before signing; agreement in Bahasa Indonesia. [F-08 · REG-030 · Permendag 71/2019 Art. 5-7]
- Domestic goods are to be prioritised; no percentage quota is stated. [F-06 · REG-029 · PP 35/2024 Art. 26-27]
- The PP names foreign franchisors and franchisees of foreign franchises as regulated parties. [F-07 · REG-029 · PP 35/2024 Art. 1(1), 3(a), 3(e)]

### 2.3 Route (a): PT PMA, own outlet

Text: the investor must incorporate a PT under Indonesian law [E-07 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 23(11)], meet the IDR 10bn plan and IDR 2.5bn paid-up capital, hold NIB and Sertifikat Standar, and be halal-certified as the operator. Tax is PBJT 10%, CIT 22%, and treaty withholding: 10% or 15% on dividends, 10% on any brand royalty paid to a Swiss entity, 5% on service fees (all in 2.2).

Analysis: this route has the most control and the highest capital need. The 2-digit rule means a second outlet in the same city does not add a second IDR 10bn base [E-04 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 26(3)(b)], but the first outlet must clear it.

### 2.4 Route (b): franchise or licence to a local partner

Text: the franchisor needs an STPW and must pass Art. 4(5); the local operator needs an STPW, holds the NIB, the halal certificate and the PBJT duty, and pays royalties subject to 20% withholding without a treaty and 10% under it (all in 2.2). Row F-07 records that a franchise to a local licensee does not trigger the PT PMA investment threshold. [F-07 · REG-029 · PP 35/2024 Art. 1(1)]

Analysis: the threshold does not apply if the operator is a wholly Indonesian-owned PT; the texts read do not state the foreign-share cut-off for PMA status (question 3). Whether a pure trademark and know-how licence without a business system falls outside "Waralaba" is not settled by the text (question 8). The halal burden and the brand-quality risk sit with a third party but remain a brand risk for CORSA.

### 2.5 Route (c): joint venture (PT with a local shareholder)

Text: a legal entity "may be classed" PMDN or PMA, and the PMA definition is deferred to investment-law rules. [E-07 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 23(9), 1(21)] No relief was found for a PMA that has a local partner. [E-04 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 26-27] The unit that applies the threshold is the PT, with paid-up capital of IDR 2.5bn per PT. [E-03 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 26(10)]

Analysis: unless a lawyer confirms that a minority foreign stake keeps the PT outside PMA, a JV is treated like route (a) for the threshold. It shares the capital, not the rule. Dividends to a Swiss shareholder below 25% direct holding would bear the 15% treaty rate. [T-10 · REG-024 · Treaty Art. 10(2)(b)]

## 3. What this means for CORSA's single-outlet entry assumption

Analysis: the CHF 200k frame does not carry a PT PMA. The plan converts IDR 10bn to about CHF 463k (plan 2 figure; not re-verified), which is about 2.3 times the frame. The text counts the plan excluding land and building, so the working-capital element helps [E-06 · REG-001 · Permen 5/2025 Art. 37(4)(e)]; whether lease fit-out, deposits and pre-opening costs count is open (question 4).

Three options follow, none legally cleared. (1) Raise capital above the threshold and open one flagship. (2) License or franchise to an Indonesian operator, accepting the PP 35/2024 gate and a royalty income model; this is the **recommended route to test first**. (3) A JV that funds the threshold jointly. Halal costs are small next to the threshold (BPJPH IDR 12,500,000 per certificate plus the LPH fee), so halal drives the kitchen design and the menu more than the budget: a mixed kitchen is not supported by the text.

The Perpres rewrite could change foreign-ownership rules but there is no sign it would change the Permen 5/2025 threshold. I treat it as an upside option, not as a plan.
