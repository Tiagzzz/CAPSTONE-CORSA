## 1. Summary (as of 2026-10-17; re-check BPJPH on 19-20 Oct 2026, because the halal phase for micro and small businesses starts on 18 Oct 2026)

| Barrier | Effect on the frame (one outlet, about CHF 200k) |
|---|---|
| Investment plan above IDR 10bn | Analysis: about 2.3 times the frame (section 3) |
| Halal duty | Day one if a PMA counts as large (question 12) |
| Franchise profit test | Gate for route (b); 5-year rule unresolved |

Deal-breakers:

1. **Investment threshold.** A PMA must plan investment above IDR 10bn excluding land and building. The test is on the investment plan, not equity alone; financing sources include loans. [E-06 · REG-001 · Art. 37(1)(c), 37(5)] For food and beverage services the base is the first 2 KBLI digits per "location point", meaning per kabupaten/kota, not per outlet. [E-04 · REG-001 · Art. 26(2), 26(3)(b), 26(4)] Repeated in PP 28/2025. [E-05 · REG-002 · Art. 212(3)(b)] Missing the limit is a sanction ground [E-04 · REG-001 · Art. 364(3)(h)]; no single-outlet, SME or phasing relief was found. Paid-up capital: at least IDR 2.5bn per PT (about CHF 116k at an undated, unsourced Plan 2 rate). [E-03 · REG-001 · Art. 26(10)]
2. **Halal is mandatory for medium and large restaurants.** The phase ended 17 Oct 2024 [H-02 · REG-009 · Art. 160(1)]; "obligation from 18 Oct 2024" is BPJPH's reading of that article, not its wording. [H-02 · REG-010 · Oct 2024] A PMA is a large business in the investment rules. [E-07 · REG-001 · Art. 26(1)] Analysis: if a PMA also counts as large for halal (no text says so; Art. 98(2) defers the scale test to other laws; question 12), the duty applies from day one. [H-03 · REG-009 · Art. 98(2)] Halal place and tools must be separated from non-halal ones, including serving; no text on certifying a corner of a mixed kitchen was found. [H-06 · REG-009 · Art. 6(4), 20, 21, 22(3)]
3. **Franchise profit test.** A franchisor needs 3 consecutive years of the franchised business plus 2 audited profitable years. [F-04 · REG-029 · Art. 4(5)] Micro and small franchisors are exempt from the audit only; whether a Swiss start-up counts as small is a question. [F-04 · REG-029 · Art. 4(6)] Analysis: a young concept may fail the 3 years. Permendag 71/2019, still "Berlaku", says 5 years. [F-05 · REG-030 · Art. 2(3)] Kontan (30 Jun 2025, tier 3) reports a minister's statement that Permendag 26/2025 revoked 36/2007, "22 Tahun 2006" as Kontan prints it (22/2016 per the F-05 row), 25/2020 and 4/2023. Kontan does not mention 71/2019; this absence rests on a minister's oral list, not a revoking article, so which test applies is a lawyer question (question 6). [F-05 · REG-901 · 30 Jun 2025]
4. **Watch item.** A new investment Perpres is in a one-year drafting programme from 22 Dec 2025; none was found. [E-02 · REG-007 · Diktum Kedua, via DDTC, tier 3]

**Not barriers:** restaurants are open to foreign investors, no cap found [E-01 · REG-003 · Art. 3(1)(d), 3(2)]; the permit route is NIB plus Sertifikat Standar (risk level and lead time unconfirmed) [E-09 · REG-002 · Art. 131, 132]; PBJT and CIT rates are in section 2.0b.

Analysis: the lowest hurdle is a franchise or licence to an Indonesian-owned operator, since the PMA threshold is not expected to attach to it (question 3). A local master licensee that sub-franchises a foreign franchise is a sub-franchisor (Art. 3(c)) and would carry the Art. 26(1), (3), (5) duties (section 2.2). An own PT PMA outlet needs a plan above IDR 10bn, about CHF 463k (same Plan 2 rate), against the CHF 200k frame; equity is at least IDR 2.5bn and the rest may be financed (question 4b).

### 2.2 What the instruments say

**Investment and capital**
- The plan OSS validates for a PMA covers at least land, building, machinery/equipment, other investment and working capital for one turnover (wages, rent, operating costs); financing sources are own capital, loans, retained profit and share premium; the text does not say a loan-funded plan satisfies Art. 26 (question 4b). [E-06 · REG-001 · Art. 37(3)-(5)]
- Paid-up capital stays in the company account for at least 12 months, except for assets, construction or operations. [E-03 · REG-001 · Art. 27(1)]
- KBLI 56101 is food provision in a fixed building; 56303 is rumah minum/kafe. [E-12 · REG-005 · Peraturan BPS 7/2025] That one 2-digit group covers both is my reading (Analysis; question 2).

**Licences**
- Medium-high risk needs NIB plus a verified Sertifikat Standar (an unverified one is only a basis for preparation). [E-09 · REG-002 · Art. 132(1)-(6)]
- Hukumonline (tier 3): KBLI 56101, large scale (PT PMA), medium-high risk; annex not opened. [E-10 · REG-008 · Lampiran I.L]
- SLHS, a tourism certificate and RPTKA: tier-3 leads. [E-11 · REG-008 · Art. 170(e)]
- A tenant in a mall whose owner holds KKPR, PL, PBG and SLF may use them; OSS verifies within 5 working days. No total lead time or fee found. [E-13 · REG-001 · Art. 41, 42]

**Halal**
- Food, drink, processing and serving must be certified. [H-01 · REG-009 · Art. 2(1), 155, 159(2)(a)]
- Self-declaration exists only for micro and small businesses with low-risk products. [H-03 · REG-009 · Art. 98(1), 98(2)] The BPJPH free scheme SEHATI (code SEHATI25) ran "Sepanjang tahun 2025 selama kuota masih tersedia"; not assumed to be running now; a 2026 or 2027 round was not checked. [H-03 · REG-015 · BPJPH FAQ, undated]
- Products from forbidden ingredients are exempt but must carry a non-halal statement. [H-05 · REG-009 · Art. 2(2), 2(3), 110(1)]
- BPJPH guideline for restaurants (incl. franchise restaurants and branches): separation in every kitchen and outlet, yearly internal audit, 6-monthly report. [H-07 · REG-018 · Bab II A.2(a), B.3(b), B.5]
- LPH fee: unit cost ceiling row 17 (food and drink with processing) IDR 3,687,500 for medium, large and foreign, times 4 to 7 mandays by number of products; row 47 IDR 5,275,000 times 2 to 5 mandays; a unit cost, not a total. Plus operational ceiling (large IDR 1,440,000; foreign 1,920,000) and transport and allowance (DKI IDR 170,000 and 210,000 per manday). The LPH picks the row; the total needs a quote (GAP). [H-09 · REG-017 · Lampiran IV]
- For breach of Art. 2(1): written warning and withdrawal of the product from circulation; certificate revocation and the IDR 2bn fine ceiling do not attach to it. [H-11 · REG-009 · Art. 170(2), 170(7), 171(2)-(5)] BPJPH says closure applies to medium and large restaurants (a BPJPH reading). [H-11 · REG-010 · BPJPH statements]
- BPJPH: no postponement of 18 Oct 2026; special provisions for UMK and imports in drafting (press statement). [H-04 · REG-012 · 9 Oct 2026]
- Imports: the Minister fixes the duty by 17 Oct 2026; no decision found. [H-12 · REG-009 · Art. 160(3)]

**Tax**
- A restaurant has at least tables, chairs and/or utensils. [T-02 · REG-019 · Art. 45(1)(a)] The base is the payment received. [T-03 · REG-019 · Art. 51(1)(a)]
- A 20% reduction applied to Aug-Dec 2025 and expired on 31 Jan 2026. [T-06 · REG-021 · Diktum KESATU(b), KELIMA] March 2026 is per DDTC only. [T-07 · REG-022 · relay]
- CIT, withholding, royalty, service fee, dividend and VAT rates: see section 2.0b. The 1988 treaty text alone says 12.5% for royalties; the later instrument prevails. [T-12 · REG-024 · Treaty Art. 12(2)]

**Franchise**
- A foreign franchisor adds a legalised home licence and a continuity statement from the Indonesian trade attache; its franchisee gets the STPW centrally. [F-03 · REG-029 · Art. 13(3), 14(3), 15(2)]
- Regional STPW: at most 5 working days, profit test unchanged; the central STPW of a foreign-franchise franchisee has an unknown lead time (question 9). [F-09 · REG-031 · Art. 4(11), 4(12)]
- Prospectus 2 weeks before signing; agreement in Bahasa Indonesia. [F-08 · REG-030 · Art. 5, 6, 7]
- Domestic goods are to be prioritised; no quota is stated. [F-06 · REG-029 · Art. 26, 27] Art. 26(1), (3), (5) bind domestic franchisors and sub-franchisors, including sub-franchisors of a foreign franchise (Art. 3(b)-(d)), not a foreign franchisor (Art. 3(a)); Art. 26(2), (4) bind franchisees (Art. 3(e)-(h)). Art. 26(2) is conditional on the franchisor's written quality standards; (4) and (5) depend on the franchisor's requirements. [F-06 · REG-029 · Art. 3(b)-(d), 26, 27, from opened text, V-P2b]
- Foreign franchisors and their franchisees are regulated parties. [F-07 · REG-029 · Art. 1(1), 3(a), 3(e)]

### 2.3 Route (a): PT PMA, own outlet

Text: a PT under Indonesian law is required (section 2.0); the investment, licence, halal and tax rows all apply to it.

Analysis: most control, highest capital need. A second outlet in the same city adds no second IDR 10bn base, but the first must clear it.

### 2.4 Route (b): franchise or licence to a local partner

Text: franchisor and operator need an STPW and the franchisor must pass the profit test. Fees to a Swiss licensor may be royalties or service fees (question 17; section 2.0b). A franchise is a right to use an intellectual-property or business system. [F-07 · REG-029 · Art. 1(1)]

Analysis: a local master licensee that sub-franchises a foreign franchise is a sub-franchisor (Art. 3(c)) and would carry the Art. 26(1), (3), (5) duties (domestic goods, local MSME suppliers and franchisees). The operator would hold the NIB, halal certificate and PBJT duty (inference). That a franchise to a local licensee does not trigger the PMA threshold rests on a worker note in F-07, not on Art. 1(1) (only the definition), and only if the operator is Indonesian-owned; the PMA foreign-share cut-off is not stated in the texts read (question 3). Whether a pure trademark and know-how licence falls outside "Waralaba" is open (question 8). The halal and quality burden sits with a third party but remains a brand risk.

### 2.5 Route (c): joint venture

Text: an entity "may be classed" PMDN or PMA; the PMA definition is deferred to investment-law rules. [E-07 · REG-001 · Art. 23(9), 1(21)] No relief was found for a PMA with a local partner. [E-04 · REG-001 · Art. 26]

Analysis: unless a lawyer confirms that a minority foreign stake keeps the PT outside PMA, a JV meets the threshold like route (a); it shares the capital, not the rule. 

## 3. What this means for the single-outlet assumption

Analysis: the CHF 200k frame does not carry a PT PMA. The plan converts IDR 10bn to about CHF 463k (undated, unsourced Plan 2 rate; no sourced dated rate was opened), about 2.3 times the frame. Equipment, other investment (incl. pre-opening operating costs during construction) and one turnover of working capital count; only land and building are excluded; leasehold fit-out and deposits are open (question 4). [E-06 · REG-001 · Art. 37(4)(a)-(e), 26(2)] Financing may be own capital, loans, retained profit or share premium [E-06 · REG-001 · Art. 37(5)]; whether shareholder loans qualify is open (question 4b).

Analysis: three options, none legally cleared: (1) raise capital above the threshold and open one flagship; (2) **license or franchise to an Indonesian operator, the route to test first**, accepting the PP 35/2024 gate and royalty income instead of outlet profit; (3) a JV that funds the threshold jointly. Halal certificate costs are small next to the threshold, but separation drives kitchen design and menu: a mixed kitchen is not supported by the text. The Perpres rewrite is an upside option only; nothing suggests it lowers the threshold.
