# Sections 9–12 — Draft for CORSA Jakarta Insights (Phase 1)

## 9. Key risks and facts pointing the other way

**Why we looked.** A Jakarta flagship can look attractive in aggregate while the addressable customer, frequency and foreign-brand evidence point in the opposite direction. The following are verified counter-signals; they are not a forecast of CORSA sales.

**Budgets and real wages.** Indonesia’s middle-class population fell from 47.9 million in 2024 to 46.7 million in 2025, while the aspiring-middle group grew to 142.0 million ([Kontan relaying Mandiri Institute](https://nasional.kontan.co.id/news/jumlah-kelas-menengah-turun-lebih-dalam-di-2025-tertekan-daya-beli-dan-konsumsi), P1C2-01/P1C2-02; N-D). Middle-class per-capita consumption grew 4.1% in 2025, but its food category grew only 0.9% while non-food grew 6.4% ([Kontan relaying Mandiri Institute](https://nasional.kontan.co.id/news/jumlah-kelas-menengah-turun-lebih-dalam-di-2025-tertekan-daya-beli-dan-konsumsi), P1C2-08/P1C2-09; N-D). South Jakarta’s total expenditure is high, but its food share fell from 38.08% in 2024 as non-food absorbed more of the increase ([Databoks relaying BPS](https://databoks.katadata.co.id/layanan-konsumen-kesehatan/statistik/721e41cd0521a99/pengeluaran-per-kapita-kota-jakarta-selatan), P1C1-F04; South Jakarta/2024/D). **Analysis:** CORSA must win a specific occasion and repeat reason; “Jakarta is affluent” is not a sufficient demand case.

The World Bank reports that workers earning middle-class wages fell from 14.5% in 2018 to 7.1% in 2025; median real wages fell about 1% for medium-skilled and 2% for high-skilled workers per year over the same period ([World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099060926111525068/pdf/P513006-58b80446-5646-4a53-b39c-800d0fa99b73.pdf), P1C2-10/P1C2-11; N-D). **Analysis:** this is a direct risk to a white-collar lunch proposition, even though Jakarta’s upper tier may remain resilient.

**Savings and eating-out restraint.** The middle-group Mandiri Saving Index was 100.4 in February 2026, its lowest since October 2023 ([CNBC Indonesia relaying Mandiri Spending Index](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/research/20260317113109-128-719750/kelas-menengah-royal-belanja-jelang-lebaran-tapi-tabungannya-menipis), P1C2-33; N-D). The highest household-expenditure group saved 16.9% of income in September 2026 versus 14.4% a year earlier ([Bank Indonesia](https://www.bi.go.id/id/publikasi/laporan/Documents/SK-September-2026.pdf), P1C2-18; U-D). Restaurant card spending still grew 4.7% year-on-year, but slower than the 6.1% comparison cited for the prior period ([Kontan relaying Bank Mandiri](https://nasional.kontan.co.id/news/mandiri-spending-index-belanja-masyarakat-melambat-keuangan-kelas-bawah-tertekan), P1C2-29; N-D). A YouGov relay reports dining out among categories cut by 23% of Gen X+ respondents ([Marketing-Interactive relaying YouGov](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/study-68-of-indonesians-hopeful-amid-financial-strains-but-spending-splits-by-generation), P4W9-28; N-D), while 66% of urban Millennials named cooking at home as a money-saving strategy ([IDN Research Institute](https://cdn.gnfi.net/goodstats/uploads/publications/files/2025/08/27/indonesia-millennial-and-gen-z-report-2025-vvhOWWB6M6.pdf), P4W9-16; U-C). **Analysis:** a lower-priced coffee and drink ladder, smaller bundles and measurable repeat are risk controls, not optional merchandising.

**Foreign-brand performance and boycotts.** Analysis: foreign and foreign-branded operators show no single outcome. FAST/KFC reported 9M 2025 revenue down 0.76% and a net loss of IDR239.58bn, versus IDR557.08bn in 9M 2024; its reporting also records 19 outlets closed and about 400 employees affected to September ([CNBC Indonesia](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/market/20251103181906-17-681892/tutup-20-gerai-pangkas-1041-karyawan-rugi-kfc--fast--turun-57), Q6-001/Q6-002; N-D), ([CNBC Indonesia](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/market/20251003175848-17-672723/masih-rugi--fast--tutup-19-gerai-phk-400-karyawan-tahun-ini), Q6-004; N-D). MAP Boga’s 9M 2025 sales fell 2.77% to IDR2.35tn and its net loss widened to IDR108.69bn; management said Starbucks had been removed from the boycott list and recovery was ongoing ([MAP Boga filing](https://www.mbai.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/9M-2025-MBA.pdf), Q6-006; N-D), ([CNBC Indonesia](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/market/20251105153124-17-682591/rugi-mapb-bengkak-jadi-rp108-m-starbucks-sudah-ditinggal-warga-ri), Q6-006; N-D). Conversely, Pizza Hut returned to IDR24.75bn profit in FY2025 from an IDR72.83bn loss, with sales around IDR3.05tn versus IDR2.79tn ([Detik Finance](https://finance.detik.com/bursa-dan-valas/d-8453630/beda-nasib-pizza-hut-kfc-satu-untung-satu-buntung), Q6-007/Q6-008; N-D). **Analysis:** these are operator outcomes, not proof that boycott caused or did not cause any one result; they show why CORSA should not extrapolate from a single brand.

Boycott sentiment is a reputational watchpoint: 80.2% agreed with boycotting Israel-affiliated products in a self-selected Indonesian online survey ([GoodStats relaying Median](https://goodstats.id/article/kenapa-publik-ri-setuju-untuk-boikot-produk-israel-n84Sj), S-04; Indonesia/2025/D), and 73.1% agreed in a five-city, non-Jakarta survey ([RMOL relaying PKAEN](https://rmol.id/nusantara/read/2025/05/15/666584/), S-03; five Indonesian cities/2025/D). **Analysis:** neither figure estimates Jakarta prevalence or CORSA’s exposure. CORSA should make ownership, sourcing, halal status and local partnership legible, and monitor sentiment before launch.

## 10. Recommendations and strategic implications

**Keep — Analysis:** keep the compact flagship thesis, the coffee-plus-food third-place idea, community partnerships and an Indonesian operating network. The concept should retain Swiss/Italian distinctiveness, but the entry case must be a South/Central Jakarta test rather than a city-wide rollout.

**Adapt — Analysis:** adapt the economics around frequency: lead with coffee and functional drinks, offer a clear entry bundle, keep pasta quality and a small number of fast halal recipes, and make value visible in dine-in and delivery. Adapt the operating route toward an Indonesian partner or licensee while counsel checks franchise, STPW, PMA, tax and control boundaries. Treat local sourcing and halal documentation as customer-facing trust assets.

**Test — Analysis:** test three linked uncertainties before committing to a permanent fit-out: (1) paid covers and net ticket by daypart, (2) repeat purchase and bundle mix, and (3) site-level conversion in SCBD/Senopati against a lower-cost lifestyle corridor. The model’s base break-even is 136.71 guests/day and its base net spend is IDR103,375 (`Scenarios!D45` and `Scenarios!D8`; model v0.6). These are decision references, not observed demand.

**Decision gates — Analysis:**

- **Go — Analysis:** proceed to a flagship only if a cleared operator/legal route and halal operating design exist, and a paid pilot reaches or beats the model base threshold (at least 137 guests/day at approximately IDR103,375 net spend) with positive contribution after verified staffing, rent, COGS and channel costs.
- **Adapt — Analysis:** continue testing if demand is between the optimistic and base break-even references (about 100–137 guests/day; `Scenarios!E45`/`D45`) or if drinks repeat but pasta does not. Change site, menu, bundle, hours or partner before signing a larger lease.
- **Stop — Analysis:** stop the Jakarta flagship path if two corridor/daypart tests remain below the optimistic break-even reference, repeat is not improving, or counsel cannot clear the operating structure and halal/import requirements within the approved capital frame. Preserve the brand option; do not fund a loss-making proof of optimism.

## 11. Ninety-day validation roadmap

**Days 1–30 — customer and proposition — Analysis:** recruit Jakarta residents and workers across 25–39 professionals, affluent social diners and run/padel communities; use intercepts near shortlisted sites, short online surveys and five-minute exit interviews. Ask about last three eating-out occasions, acceptable coffee/drink and pasta prices, frequency, dietary/halal requirements, delivery use, and what would make them return. Run menu tasting with two price ladders and record paid—not stated—choice, service time, waste and repeat intent.

**Days 31–60 — location and operating proof — Analysis:** count passers-by, seated guests, queue length, competitor covers and delivery pickups at SCBD, Senopati, Kuningan and one lower-cost lifestyle corridor across weekday lunch, weekday evening and weekend dayparts. The model’s district footfall fields are blank and its demand indices are judgement inputs (`Location!H8:N13`; model v0.6). Obtain at least two deal-rent quotes per finalist, verify usable ground-floor area, drainage/flood access, permits, service charges and halal kitchen constraints.

**Days 61–90 — paid pilot and decision — Analysis:** use a pop-up, partner counter or short event series rather than a long lease. Track daily paid covers, net ticket, drink/pasta mix, channel contribution, repeat within 30 days, acquisition source, refunds, prep time and waste. Reconcile observed results to the model and re-run the three gates in Section 10. **Assumption:** customer consented QRIS/loyalty identifiers can support repeat measurement; if not, use anonymous receipt cohorts and transparent opt-in.

**Primary-research brief — Analysis:** the customer module should interview customers, non-customers and venue operators; the location module should interview brokers, mall/corridor managers, delivery riders and comparable cafés; the legal module should interview an Indonesian counsel and halal adviser. Use structured scripts, quota the three priority occasions, preserve verbatim objections, and pre-register the decision rule before seeing results. No interview should be presented as market prevalence.
