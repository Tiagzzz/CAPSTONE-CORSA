### A.1 Political (5 Core Verified Factors)

1. **Open Foreign Ownership on Paper (No Equity Cap Found for Restaurants/Cafés):**
   - **Fact & Value:** Restaurants (`KBLI 56101`) and cafés (`KBLI 56303`) are not on any priority, MSME-reserved, partnership-required, or conditional investment list under `Perpres 10/2021` (set 2 Feb 2021) as amended by `Perpres 49/2021` (`Art. 3(1)(d), 3(2)`); default status is open to all investors with no foreign-ownership cap found ([Presiden RI / Setneg via JDIH BKPM](https://jdih-storage.bkpm.go.id/jdih/userfiles/documents/2023/10/2021._10_Perpres_Bidang_Usaha_Penanaman_Modal_.pdf), [JDIH Kemenko Infra](https://jdih.kemenkoinfra.go.id/dokumen/file/perpres/2021/salinan-perpres-nomor-49-tahun-2021.pdf), [Hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/klinik/a/bisakah-pt-pma-mendirikan-usaha-restoran--lt6a3d0cc52dc29/), `E-01`; `N-D`).
   - **CORSA Implication:** No statutory political/equity barrier forces a local partner; partnership is a commercial and capital-threshold choice, though the absence of KBLI 56 in the scanned annexes rests on OCR and requires legal confirmation.
   - **Confidence:** `Medium` (annexes read via OCR; Hukumonline is Tier 3 relay printed 25 Jun 2026).

2. **Active Regulatory & Policy Flux (Investment List & Halal Special Provisions):**
   - **Fact & Value:** `Keppres 38/2025` (set and effective 22 Dec 2025, `Diktum Kedua`) established a 1-year drafting programme for a new Presidential Regulation on investment fields; none had been issued by 17 Oct 2026 ([DDTCNews](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/nasional/1817066/pemerintah-akan-perbarui-perpres-terkait-bidang-usaha-penanaman-modal), `E-02`; `N-D`, relay). Meanwhile, BPJPH stated on Friday, 9 Oct 2026 that the 18 Oct 2026 mandatory halal start date for micro/small businesses and expanded categories is not postponed, while "special provisions" (*ketentuan khusus*) for MSEs and imports are being drafted ([BPJPH](https://bpjph.halal.go.id/main/read/wajib-halal-berlaku-18-oktober-2026-bpjph-siapkan-aturan-yang-memudahkan-pelaku-usaha), `H-04`; `N-D`).
   - **CORSA Implication:** Rules governing foreign investment fields and imported F&B inputs can shift inside CORSA's 90-day planning horizon; `H-04` and `H-12` require immediate post-18 Oct 2026 re-verification.
   - **Confidence:** `Medium` (`E-02` is Tier 3 relay; `H-04` is a press statement whose drafted provisions are unpublished).

   - **Fact & Value:** FY2025 total realised investment reached IDR 1,931.2 trillion (`+12.7%` YoY), of which foreign investment (PMA) was IDR 900.9 trillion (`+0.1%` YoY) ([Kementerian Investasi dan Hilirisasi/BKPM](https://www.bkpm.go.id/id/info/siaran-pers/realisasi-investasi-2025-lampaui-target-hilirisasi-melompat-43-3-persen), [Kontan](https://nasional.kontan.co.id/news/naik-127-realisasi-investasi-tahun-2025-tembus-rp-19312-triliun), `S-09`; `N-D`). In H1 2026 (Jan–Jun 2026 cumulative), total realised investment reached IDR 1,010.6 trillion (`+7.2%` YoY) ([BKPM](https://bkpm.go.id/id/info/siaran-pers/realisasi-investasi-semester-i-2026-tembus-rp-1-010-t-serap-1-4-juta-tenaga-kerja-langsung), [Katadata](https://katadata.co.id/amp/berita/nasional/6a588db0af3cf/realisasi-investasi-semester-i-tembus-rp1010-t-mayoritas-dari-modal-asing), [DDTCNews](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/nasional/1820896/realisasi-investasi-semester-i2026-tembus-rp10106-triliun), `S-10`; `N-D`).

### A.6 Legal (5 Core Verified Factors)

1. **PT PMA Minimum Investment Plan (>IDR 10bn) & Paid-Up Capital (IDR 2.5bn):**
   - **Fact & Value:** Under `Permen Investasi/BKPM 5/2025` (set 1 Oct 2025, promulgated 2 Oct 2025, *"Berlaku"*) and `PP 28/2025` (set 5 Jun 2025, *"Berlaku"*), a foreign-owned company (`PT PMA`) is classified as a large enterprise (`Art. 26(1)`) and must plan total investment `above IDR 10 billion` excluding land and building per 2-digit KBLI (`56` food & beverage services) per location point (`per kabupaten/kota`, `Art. 26(2), 26(3)(b), 26(4)`), plus paid-up capital of at least `IDR 2.5 billion` per PT locked for 12 months except for assets, construction, or operations (`Art. 26(10), 27(1)`) ([JDIH BKPM Permen 5/2025](https://jdih-storage.bkpm.go.id/jdih/jdih/2025Permeninvesthil005-.pdf), [JDIH BKPM PP 28/2025](https://jdih-storage.bkpm.go.id/jdih/jdih/Salinan-PP-Nomor-28-Tahun-2025-.pdf), `E-03`, `E-04`, `E-05`, `E-07`; `N-D`).
   - **CORSA Implication:** At `>IDR 10bn` (~2.3x the team's ~CHF 200k single-outlet budget frame), a wholly owned single-outlet PT PMA is financially out of reach unless multi-outlet/debt-financed or entered via a local partner/licensee.
   - **Confidence:** `High` (Tier 1 primary regulations in force).

2. **Mandatory Halal Certification & Strict Kitchen/Serving Separation:**
   - **Fact & Value:** Under `PP 42/2024` (set and promulgated 17 Oct 2024, `Art. 2(1), 6(4), 20–22, 160`), food, drink, processing, and serving services must be halal-certified (`H-01`; `N-D`); medium and large businesses have been in the mandatory phase since `18 Oct 2024` (after the phase ended 17 Oct 2024, `H-02`; `N-D`), and micro/small businesses enter on `18 Oct 2026` (`H-02`; `N-D`). Halal facilities and tools must be physically separated from non-halal ones across storage, processing, and serving in every kitchen and outlet (`H-06`, `H-07`; `N-D`). BPJPH's certificate tariff is `IDR 12.5 million` for large or foreign-origin businesses (plus LPH inspection unit cost of `IDR 3,687,500 x 4–7 mandays` under `Kepkaban 22/2024`) ([Sumut JDIH PP 42/2024](https://jdih.sumutprov.go.id/assets/reg/1735286333884_PP_NOMOR_42_TAHUN_2024.pdf), [BPJPH Kepkaban 22/2024](https://cmsbl.halal.go.id/uploads/Kepkaban_22_2024_Perubahan_IV_Kepkaban_141_Tahun_2021_9a2ede13b7.pdf), [BPJPH Kepkaban 78/2023](https://cmsbl.halal.go.id/uploads/78_2023_SK_Pedoman_Sertifikasi_Halal_Makanan_dan_Minuman_dengan_Pengelolahan_3aa915efdf.pdf), `H-01`, `H-02`, `H-06`, `H-07`, `H-08`, `H-09`; `N-D`).
   - **CORSA Implication:** A "halal corner" in a mixed pork/alcohol kitchen is unsupported by legal text; CORSA must commit to a fully halal-certified kitchen from day one (especially if a PMA is treated as "large" for halal, an open legal step in `H-03`).
   - **Confidence:** `High` for statutory dates, separation rule, and BPJPH tariff (`H-01`, `H-02`, `H-06`, `H-08`); `Medium` for whether investment-law "large" classification carries over to halal (`H-03`) and for `H-04` / `H-12` pending rules.

3. **Franchise Registration (STPW) & Conflicting 3-Year vs 5-Year Track-Record Gate:**
   - **Fact & Value:** `PP 35/2024` (set and promulgated 2 Sep 2024, replacing `PP 42/2007`, `Art. 4(5), 12–15`) requires both franchisor and franchisee to hold a franchise registration certificate (`STPW`) and requires the franchisor to have `3 consecutive years` of operation plus `2 audited profitable years` (micro/small exempt from audit only, `Art. 4(6)`), while prioritising domestic goods (`Art. 26–27`, no numerical quota) ([JDIH Kemendag PP 35/2024](https://jdih.kemendag.go.id/peraturan/peraturan-pemerintah-nomor-35-tahun-2024-tentang-waralaba-1), `F-01`, `F-02`, `F-04`, `F-06`, `F-07`; `N-D`). However, `Permendag 71/2019` (`Art. 2(3)`), still listed as *"Berlaku"* on JDIH, requires `5 years` of business experience ([JDIH Kemendag Permendag 71/2019](https://jdih.kemendag.go.id/peraturan/peraturan-menteri-perdagangan-nomor-71-tahun-2019-penyelenggaraan-waralaba), [Kontan](https://nasional.kontan.co.id/news/empat-permendag-dicabut-izin-usaha-waralaba-kini-lebih-cepat), `F-05`; `N-D`).
   - **CORSA Implication:** While franchising/licensing to an Indonesian partner avoids the IDR 10bn PMA threshold, a young Swiss brand may fail the `3+2 year` (or `5-year`) profitability/experience gate unless structured as a non-franchise IP/know-how licence (`L11–L13`).
   - **Confidence:** `Medium` in PESTEL summary (`High` on the statutory texts of `PP 35/2024` and `Permendag 71/2019` existing; unresolved conflict between them).

4. **Jakarta Restaurant Tax (PBJT 10%), CIT (22%), VAT (11%/12%), & Swiss Treaty Withholding:**
   - **Fact & Value:** Jakarta levies a `10%` restaurant tax (`PBJT`) on food and drink sales (`Perda DKI 1/2024` set/promulgated 5 Jan 2024, `Art. 45(2)(a), 53(1)`, exempting turnover $\le\text{IDR 42m/month}$); temporary 20% reductions (`Kepgub 722/2025` for Aug–Dec 2025 expired 31 Jan 2026; `Kepgub 310/2026` for Mar 2026 only) have expired ([Bapenda DKI Jakarta](https://bapenda.jakarta.go.id/peraturan-perpajakan/unduh/perda-nomor-1-tahun-2024-tentang-pajak-dan-retribusi-daerah), [JDIH DKI Jakarta](https://jdih.jakarta.go.id/dokumenPeraturanDirectory/0031/2025KEPGUB0031722.pdf), [DDTCNews](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/daerah/1818539/gubernur-dki-beri-diskon-pajak-restoran-dan-hotel-hingga-20-persen), `T-01`, `T-04`, `T-06`, `T-07`; `J-D`). Corporate income tax (CIT) is `22%` (`11%` on turnover up to IDR 4.8bn per DJP summary) ([DJP](https://pajak.go.id/sites/default/files/2026-07/Aspek%20Perpajakan%20WP%20Baru%20Terdaftar%20%28Badan%20dan%20OP%29.pdf), `T-08`; `N-D`). Under the Indonesia–Switzerland tax treaty (1988 Agreement + 2007 Protocol), cross-border withholding ceilings are `10%` (at $\ge 25\%$ direct holding) or `15%` for dividends (`T-10`), `10%` for royalties (`T-11`), and `5%` for technical/managerial service fees (`T-13`), vs `20%` statutory PPh 26 (`T-09`) ([DJP Treaty Text](https://pajak.go.id/sites/default/files/2025-05/Tax%20Treaty%20Swiss%20%28Modified%20by%20MLI%29%20-%20EN%20.pdf), [DJP Rates](https://pajak.go.id/index.php/en/tax-treaty-rates), [DJP Switzerland](https://www.pajak.go.id/en/p3b/switzerland), [Swiss SIF](https://www.sif.admin.ch/dam/en/sd-web/Rq53YeWNalWg/DBA-Liste%20EN-per%2001.01.2026.pdf), `T-09`, `T-10`, `T-11`, `T-13`; `N-D`). VAT is `12%` statutory on an `11/12` base (`11%` effective for non-luxury items per `PMK 131/2024` relay) ([DDTCNews](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/nasional/1807939/pmk-terbaru-soal-ppn-12-akhirnya-terbit-begini-perinciannya), `T-15`; `N-D`, relay).
   - **CORSA Implication:** Budget the full `10%` PBJT on all dine-in/takeaway menu prices and structure Swiss cross-border charges carefully between royalties (`10%` WHT) and service fees (`5%` WHT).
   - **Confidence:** `High` for PBJT 10% (`T-01`, `T-04`, `T-06`) and treaty rates (`T-08`, `T-09`, `T-10`, `T-11`, `T-13`); `Medium` in PESTEL summary for overall cross-border tax characterisation and relay rows (`T-07`, `T-15`).

5. **Active Building-Permit (PBG/SLF) Enforcement on Sport/Lifestyle Venues:**
   - **Fact & Value:** In March 2026, `185 of 397` Jakarta padel venues (courts or buildings, unit unresolved in press) lacked a building permit (`PBG`), and North Jakarta authorities sealed `2` unpermitted courts (in Ancol and Penjaringan) on 4 March 2026 ([CNA Indonesia](https://www.cna.id/indonesia/lapangan-padel-jakarta-tak-berizin-disegel-45161), [Metro TV News](https://www.metrotvnews.com/read/ba4CPmyw-dua-lapangan-padel-tak-berizin-di-ancol-dan-penjaringan-disegel), `UC-N07`, `P4W5-08`; `J-D`, relay).    - **Confidence:** `Medium` (`UC-N07`, `P4W5-08` relays; `E-13` original text, total NIB/Sertifikat Standar lead time is a gap).

### B.3 How Entering Through an Indonesian Network Reduces Administrative Distance (Gap Analysis Point B8)

Because CORSA's practical entry route leverages an **Indonesian family/operator network** rather than a cold greenfield Swiss subsidiary, administrative distance shifts across the three main entry structures (`reports/2026-10-17-cage-switzerland-jakarta.md` §3.4):

1. **Where an Indonesian-Owned Licensee / Franchisee REDUCES Administrative Distance:**
   - **Avoids the `>IDR 10bn` PMA Investment Plan & `IDR 2.5bn` Capital Threshold:** An Indonesian-owned operator (`PMDN` under `Permen 5/2025 Art. 23(8)–(9)`) is not expected to trigger the `>IDR 10bn` PMA investment threshold (`E-04`) or `IDR 2.5bn` paid-up capital lock (`E-03`) (subject to lawyer confirmation on foreign control/royalty tests, Question `L14`). *Note: A Joint Venture (`PT PMA`) with any foreign equity does NOT reduce this distance unless a lawyer confirms an exception (`L4`).*
   - **Shifts Local Licensing (`NIB`, `Sertifikat Standar`, `SLHS`, `PBG/SLF`) & Local Tax Filing (`PBJT 10%`) to the Indonesian Operator:** The local operator holds the operating entity, applies for the `NIB` and `Sertifikat Standar` (`E-09`), uses mall landlord building permits (`E-13`), and files monthly Jakarta `PBJT` (`T-01`), reducing CORSA Switzerland's direct administrative and travel burden (`CAGE-004`).
   - **Softens High Power-Distance & Indirect-Communication Friction (Analysis):** Culturally (`PD 78`, `IDV 5` [`CAGE-001`]), an established Indonesian network eases landlord negotiations, local staffing, and municipal inspections (analysis; not measured by a row).

2. **Where an Indonesian Network Shifts or ADDS Administrative Distance (Does NOT Remove It):**
   - **Halal Compliance & Brand Risk (`Shifted, Not Removed`):** The mandatory halal certificate (`H-01`, `H-02`) and strict physical separation of kitchen/storage/serving (`H-06`, `H-07`) attach to the product and outlet (`Kepkaban 78/2023 Bab II A.2(a), B.3(b)` explicitly covers franchise restaurants and branches). Even if the local operator pays the tariff and runs the kitchen, any halal failure destroys CORSA's brand.
   - **Franchise Regime & Track-Record Gate (`Added Distance`):** Licensing/franchising triggers `PP 35/2024`: both Swiss franchisor and Indonesian franchisee must obtain `STPW` (`F-02`), the Swiss franchisor must provide an apostilled home licence and Indonesian trade attaché continuity statement (`F-03`), a Bahasa Indonesia agreement and prospectus 2 weeks before signing (`F-08`), and pass the `3+2 year` audited profit test (`F-04`) or `5-year` rule (`F-05`), unless a pure IP/know-how licence falls outside *"Waralaba"* (`L13`). Furthermore, if a local master licensee sub-franchises, it becomes a *sub-franchisor* (`PP 35/2024 Art. 3(c)`) bound by `Art. 26(1), (3), (5)` to prioritise domestic goods and local MSME suppliers/franchisees (`F-06`).
   - **Cross-Border Withholding Tax & Imported Input Rules (`Unchanged/Added`):** Remitting royalties or service fees to Switzerland triggers `10%` royalty WHT (`T-11`) or `5%` service WHT (`T-13`) plus self-assessed VAT on imported intangibles (`11%` effective / `12%` statutory, `T-15`), and imported Swiss/Italian packaged ingredients still require Sihalal foreign-certificate registration (`IDR 800,000`, `H-12`, `CAGE-024`).

  - In `2026-10-17-customer-target-profiles.md`, Group 4 (*Lifestyle-driven café-goers*) was treated as an attitudinal overlay cutting across Group 1 (Young urban professionals) and Group 2 (Affluent South Jakarta residents) because survey datasets measure *third-place café motives* (gathering with friends `44%` [`UC-N13`], work/study focus `34%` [`P4W7b-01`], self-reward `49.1%` [`P4W7b-13`]) rather than a separate demographic census count.
  - To define **Four Distinct Target Groups** for Deliverable 1 without double-counting population headcounts, the four segments are distinguished by **primary occasion, catchment anchor, and basket structure**, while explicitly noting demographic overlap:
    1. **Group 1 — Young Urban CBD Professionals (Aged 25–39, Weekday Workday Routine):** Anchored in SCBD, Senopati, and Kuningan office corridors during **weekday lunch and commuter coffee occasions** (fuel, convenience, fast dine-in or app delivery).
    2. **Group 2 — Conditionally Affluent South Jakarta Upper-Middle & Upper-Class Residents (Aged 29–45+, Full-Price Dining Anchor):** Anchored in South Jakarta residential neighbourhoods and upscale malls during **evening and weekend full-meal dining occasions** (quality-led, price-resilient, peer-review-driven).
    3. **Group 3 — Active Urban Fitness, Running & Padel Community Members (Aged 21–40, Post-Session Recovery & Group Ritual):** Anchored around GBK/Senayan running routes and South Jakarta's 34+ padel clubs during **early-morning weekend runs and post-match weekday evening sessions** (functional drinks, protein shakes, light recovery snacks).
    4. **Group 4 — Aesthetic / Social "Third-Place" Lifestyle Café Seekers (Aged 18–35 Gen Z & Younger Millennials, Dwell-Time & Social/Work Occasion):** Distinguished from Groups 1–3 by their **primary occasion and motivation**: they visit cafés for **1–4 hour social gathering (`44%`), remote work/laptop sessions (`34%`), and leisure self-reward (`49.1%`)**, ordering specialty iced coffee/matcha and sharing snacks rather than a quick weekday office lunch (Group 1), a full weekend family/couple dinner (Group 2), or a post-workout recovery shake (Group 3). Demographically they overlap with younger members of Groups 1 and 2, so **their headcount must not be added on top of Groups 1 and 2 in TAM/SAM sizing**, and—crucially—the evidence shows they are **drawn by social feeds/FOMO on trial (`P4W2b-23`) but only return for taste (`83%`), room comfort (`78%`), and service (`70%`), NOT aesthetics (`27%`, last place [`P4W7b-14`])**!

### C.2 Why Students, Creators, Expats, Families, and Nightlife Were Dropped as Primary Launch Targets

1. **Students (Deferred to Entry-Price Spillover Only):**
   - **Why Dropped:** National Gen Z coffee buyers are overwhelmingly price-first: `48%` spend **under IDR 10,000 per cup** and `30%` spend IDR 10,000–25,000 (including home-made coffee), while `47%` name price as the #1 factor ([Databoks relaying Jakpat Dec 2024](https://databoks.katadata.co.id/en/consumer-services/statistics/677cd50372d4a/how-much-do-gen-z-spend-on-coffee-this-survey-reveals), `P1C3-01`, `UE-N03`, `UE-N04`; `N-B`, `Medium`). Among urban Gen Z, `56%` choose a frugal lifestyle, `60%` rely on promotions, and `54%` limit social outings ([IDN Research Institute](https://cdn.gnfi.net/goodstats/uploads/publications/files/2025/08/27/indonesia-millennial-and-gen-z-report-2025-vvhOWWB6M6.pdf), `P4W9-19`, `P4W9-14`, `P4W9-15`; `U-C`, `Low`), and only `14%` put dining out in their top 3 spend categories (`P4W9-26`; `N-D`, `Low`). Furthermore, young Javanese students prioritise comfortable seating (`75%`) and power outlets (`53%`) for long study stays ([Jakpat Insight](https://insight.jakpat.net/strong-traditional-tastes-in-the-local-fb-market/), `P4W7b-05`; `U-D`, `Low`), which clogs seat turnover during peak lunch hours at low ticket sizes. The internal brief's figure of `837,400` Jakarta students has no verified row.
2. **Content Creators / Influencers (Reclassified as an Acquisition Channel, Not a Customer Segment):**
   - **Why Dropped:** Creators are a media/acquisition channel, not a revenue-generating buyer cohort (claim `E1-06`). Even as a channel, their conversion is weak: while `40%` discover new eating places via TikTok food accounts (`P4W1-R05`; `U-D`, `Medium`) and `67%` in 6 SEA markets ever bought something recommended by a creator (`P4W2b-12`; `P-D`, `Low`), creators rank **last of three trust sources** (`1.98/4.0` vs `2.42` for family/friends and `2.36` for online reviews [`P4W2b-11`; `P-D`, `Low`]), `83%` opened an affiliate link but `<40%` bought in 6 months (`P4W2b-14`; `N-D`, `Low`), and food-vlogger trust fell after a review scandal ($n=85$, qualitative) ([Jerkin Journal](https://jerkin.org/index.php/jerkin/article/view/1265), `P4W2b-21`; `N-C`, `Low`), with micro/nano creators holding trust better than mega influencers ([Marketing-Interactive relaying impact.com x Cube 2025](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/study-76-of-indonesians-shop-through-creators-despite-declining-trust), `P4W2b-06`; `P-D`, `Low`).
3. **Expatriates (Dropped Due to Zero Verified Evidence & Small Base):**
   - **Why Dropped:** Zero verified rows size or describe expat F&B behaviour in Jakarta. The internal brief's figure of `~15,000` foreign workers in Jakarta (2024 Kemnaker via GoodStats) is unverified in the processed CSVs and too small to anchor a standalone launch strategy (claim `E1-06`). The only expat mention in the CSVs is an ambiguous Playtomic remark about Bali padel clubs ([Playtomic Global Padel Report 2025](https://8258038.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/8258038/Global%20Padel%20Report/PLAYTOMIC_GLOBAL%20_PADEL_REPORT_2025.pdf), `UC-N06`; `J-D`, `Low`).
4. **Families & Large Groups / Nightlife & After-Hours Consumers (Dropped / Not Researched):**
   - **Why Dropped:** Families scored lowest in Tiago's initial matrix (`16/30`, "test later") and have no verified rows; nightlife/after-hours consumers have zero verified rows and conflict with CORSA's daytime coffee/lunch/sport-recovery and halal-certified positioning.
