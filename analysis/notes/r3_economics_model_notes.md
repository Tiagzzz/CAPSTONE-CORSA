## Section A — Demand & Macroeconomic Interpretation

### 1. The Macro-Micro Paradox: National Middle-Class Compression vs. Jakarta Upper-Tier Bifurcation

The central macroeconomic finding of the repository is a **K-shaped structural divergence** between Indonesia's contracting national middle class and a resilient, high-spending, highly unequal urban upper tier concentrated in DKI Jakarta—and specifically in South Jakarta (`Jakarta Selatan`). Any business case for CORSA that relies on a generic "rising Indonesian middle class" narrative is empirically falsified by the 2024–2026 data; conversely, a business case anchored on **South/Central Jakarta's top expenditure deciles and corporate daytime enclaves** is supported by district-level expenditure and transaction data, provided unit economics do not depend on mass-market repeat frequency.

### 2. Prepared-Food Expenditure, Engel's Law, and the Wheat/Pasta Structural Tailwind

1. **Prepared-Food Budget Envelope (`Makanan dan Minuman Jadi`):**
   - In DKI Jakarta (Mar 2026 Susenas, `P1C1-E01` / `DEM-031`), monthly per-capita spend on prepared food and beverages reached **Rp524,958/month** (`17.1%` of total Rp3.07M expenditure and `44.1%` of total Rp1.19M food expenditure), compared to a national benchmark of **Rp248,501/month** (Mar 2025 Susenas, `P1C1-E06` / `DEM-032`).
   - At the city (`kota`) level in 2024 (`DEM-030`), prepared-food spend diverged sharply across Jakarta:
     - **Jakarta Selatan:** **Rp548,413/capita/month** (**+25.0% YoY** vs. 2023, `P1C1-E02`).
     - **Jakarta Utara:** **Rp556,691/capita/month** (**+9.4% YoY**, `P1C1-E04`, relevant for PIK comparators).
     - **Jakarta Pusat:** **Rp513,174/capita/month** (**-2.5% YoY**, below its 2020 peak of Rp521,859, `P1C1-E03`).
     - **Jakarta Barat:** **Rp454,710/capita/month** (**-5.8% YoY**, `P1C1-E05`).
   - **Engel's Law Caveat in South Jakarta (`P1C1-F03`, `P1C1-F04` / `DEM-020`):** Although South Jakarta's total per-capita expenditure surged **+15.57% YoY** in 2025 (from Rp3,533,226 to Rp4,083,229/month), food expenditure grew only **+6.09% YoY** (from Rp1,345,560 to Rp1,427,470/month), while non-food expenditure (housing, transport, lifestyle, financial/services) jumped **+21.40% YoY** (from Rp2,187,665 to Rp2,655,760/month). Consequently, the food share of total expenditure fell **-3.12 percentage points** in a single year (`38.08%` $\rightarrow$ `34.96%`). Rising top-line expenditure in South Jakarta does **not** automatically translate one-for-one into food volume; incremental wallet share is captured only when F&B is positioned as a **lifestyle, third-place, or health/social experience** rather than utility calories.

2. **Target Age Cohort Sizing vs. Daytime Commuter Gap:**
   - Verified June 2024 BPS-via-Databoks resident counts (`DEM-028`, `DEM-029`) establish a core `25–39` resident base of **535,920 persons** in South Jakarta (`22.71%` of 2.36M residents, `P1C1-S06`; `927,900` if widened to `20–44`) and **244,600 persons** in Central Jakarta (`22.80%` of 1.07M residents, `P1C1-P06`; `419,190` for `20–44`), totaling **780,520 residents aged 25–39** across the two core administrative cities (`1,347,090` aged `20–44`).
   - However, **no DKI-specific or district-specific expenditure-class cross-tabulation** exists in opened sources (only DKI's `4.14%` poverty rate in Sep 2024 is verified, `P1C1-C01` / `DEM-033`), and **no verified SCBD/Senopati daytime office-worker or commuter count** was recovered.

3. **Foodservice & Wheat/Pasta Macro Fundamentals (`wheat-foodservice.csv`):**
   - **Foodservice Market Expansion (`DEM-049`, `DEM-054`, `DEM-055`):** USDA FAS Food Service Hotel Restaurant Institutional reports confirm Indonesia's overall foodservice market grew from **USD 58.9B** (2024) to **USD 63.3B** (2025, **+7.5% YoY**), projected to reach **USD 95B by 2030** (`~8.5%` CAGR, `P1C4-01`). Crucially, full-service restaurants hold **80.7% market share** (**USD 51.1B** in 2025, projected `8.72%` CAGR 2025–2030, `P1C4-02`), while cafés/bars reached **USD 2.48B** in 2024 (`+11.7%` YoY vs. USD 2.22B in 2023) with coffee shops comprising **USD 1.9B** (`76.6%` of café/bar sales, `P1C4-04`). Full-service pizza inside the USD 51.1B segment is **USD 2.0B** (`P1C4-03`, closest western-casual sub-segment proxy).
   - **Dietary Shift Toward Wheat & Pasta (`DEM-050`, `DEM-051`, `DEM-052`, `DEM-057`–`061`):** Wheat flour consumption reached **25.3 kg/capita/year** in MY 2024/25 (`P1C4-12`), driven by young urban consumers shifting toward western/convenience foods (`P1C4-13`). While instant noodles dominate total flour use (`60–70%`), pasta accounts for `~5%` of industrial wheat usage (`P1C4-15`) and exhibits rapid import expansion: pasta imports grew **+16% YoY in 2022 to USD 36M** (`P1C4-16`), and imports of uncooked pasta (`HS 190219`) surged **+71.9% in USD** (`USD 10.67M` $\rightarrow$ `USD 18.35M`) and **+56.1% in volume** (`7,022 t` $\rightarrow$ `10,965 t`) between 2022 and 2023 (`P1C4-17` to `P1C4-20`), with Italy as the #2 supplier (**USD 3.16M**, `1,568 t`, implied landed unit value `~USD 2.01/kg` or `~Rp32,700/kg`, `P1C4-21`).

1. **Customer Acquisition (Rejecting the Promo-Driven Mass Middle, Targeting Lifestyle & Corporate Enclaves):**
   - Consumer survey data (`consumer-surveys.csv`) demonstrates that the broader urban middle and Gen Z cohorts are intensely promo-sensitive: **47%** of Gen Z coffee buyers cite price as their #1 consideration (`48%` spend `<Rp10,000` and `42%` spend `Rp10k–25k`, leaving only `10%` above Rp25,000; `P1C3-01` / `DEM-001`), **60%** of urban Java diners choose venues for affordability (`P1C3-11` / `DEM-012`), and on delivery apps **55%** use platforms primarily for promos while **67%** check discount tags before ordering (`BEH-039`).
   - If CORSA attempts to acquire customers via aggregator discount wars (`Inputs!F48` models a `20%` delivery commission on `15%` delivery share, `Inputs!F37`), it will bleed gross margin to promo-switchers who defect the moment subsidies end.
   - Instead, customer acquisition must rely on **organic enclave capture and community lock-in**: (a) physical adjacency to Grade-A offices/high-income residences in SCBD/Senopati (`25hours Hotel The Oddbird` / ASHTA District 8, Pacific Place, Setiabudi) where corporate expense accounts and top-decile salaries insulate guests from real-wage erosion; and (b) **community-led acquisition** (running crews such as RPC, WIP, Riot, IndoRunners using specialty cafés around GBK/Senopati as bag-drop and post-run social hubs, `BEH-025`–`029`, `REG-110`–`112`, where customer acquisition cost is near zero and retention is social rather than discount-driven).

2. **Purchase Frequency (Why Beverage-Led Habit Must Bridge Food-Led Occasions):**
   - **Budget Share Arithmetic:** At CORSA's model v0.6 blended average net ticket of **Rp103,375** (`Menu!C31`, or **Rp113,713 gross** including 10% PBJT; `Inputs` weighted dine-in/takeaway/delivery net spend is **Rp106,625**, `Revenue!F7`), a **single visit** absorbs **20.7%** of the average South Jakarta resident's *entire* monthly prepared-food budget (`Rp113,713 / Rp548,413`). Even for an upper-middle professional spending `3x` the city average on prepared food (`~Rp1.65M/month`), a Rp114k lunch every workday (`22x/month = Rp2.5M`) is prohibitive.
   - Therefore, **food-led visits (pasta + drink at Rp120k–150k) are structurally 1x–4x/month occasions**, even within the affluent target segment. High-frequency repeat traffic (`3x–5x/week`) can **only** be achieved through the **morning/afternoon coffee & functional-drink occasion** (Net Rp32k–58k / Gross Rp35.2k–63.8k), where a Rp40k Americano or Rp50k Latte fits into daily corporate coffee budgets (`52%` of affluent coffee drinkers consume 2–3 cups/day, and `54%` pick up morning coffee on the commute, `BEH-018` / TGM `REG-026`, `BEH-084` / Jakpat `REG-085`).

3. **Ticket-Size Architecture (Barbell / Daypart Split vs. Blended Ticket Illusion):**
   - Model v0.6 assumes **200 guests/day** in the Base Case (`Inputs!F33`) spending a blended **Rp106,625 net** (`Inputs!F36`, `F38`, `F40`), driven by a `Menu` sheet (`06-menu.csv`) that assumes **55 pasta portions + 22 breakfast/salad/snack portions + 82 drinks + 18 add-ons/desserts = 177 items per 100 guests** (`1.77 items/guest`).
   - Economically, this blended ticket masks two distinct behavioral regimes:
     - **Daypart 1 (07:00–11:00 & 14:30–17:00 — Grab-and-Go / WFC / Run-Club Beverage & Light Bite):** Ticket size **Rp40,000–75,000 net** (`1.0–1.3 items/guest`: coffee/matcha/protein shake + optional croissant/yogurt bowl).
     - **Daypart 2 (11:30–14:00 & 18:00–21:30 — Dine-In / Delivery Pasta & Drink Bundle):** Ticket size **Rp125,000–165,000 net** (`1.8–2.2 items/guest`: pasta + signature drink/matcha + side/tiramisu).
   - **Strategic Imperative:** CORSA must explicitly price and market **bundled lunch/post-workout combos** (e.g., Pasta + Iced Americano/Matcha at Rp115k–135k net) to protect the `1.77 items/guest` attachment rate in `Menu`, while ensuring morning takeaway coffee (`Inputs!F38` net Rp55,000, `25%` channel share `Inputs!F35`) is fast enough to capture high-frequency commuter volume without cannibalizing dine-in table turns during the 12:00–13:30 lunch peak.

#### A. Critical Tax-Basis & Sample-Bias Adjustment
Before comparing numbers, three structural features of the datasets must be accounted for:
1. **Tax & Service Charge Basis (`Net` vs. `++` vs. `Gross`):**
   - In CORSA Model v0.6 (`06-menu.csv`), all menu prices in `Menu!C7:C26` are explicitly **NET of 10% PBJT** (`Menu!C4: "All prices NET of 10% PBJT"`), while `Menu!N7:N26` computes the **Guest price incl. 10% PBJT** (`Net x 1.10`, with **no service charge** modelled).

By contrast, **Pizza Marzano** (`18` rows = **26.9%**, `CMP-054`), **Fore Coffee** (`14` rows = **20.9%**, `CMP-053`/`059`/`060`), **Pepper Lunch** (`3` rows, `CMP-056`), **Lawless** (`3` rows, `CMP-059`), **Kopi Tuku** (`1` row, `CMP-053`), and street-pasta kiosks (`4` rows, `CMP-058`) are drawn from media listicles where tax/service inclusion is unstated (`17` of `67` numeric rows also carry approximate language such as `sekitar`, `-an`, or `ribuan`).
2. **Sample Concentration Bias:** Three brands—**Burgreens (24), Pizza Marzano (18), and Fore Coffee (14)**—account for **56 of 67 numeric rows (83.6%)** in `price-ladder.csv`. Consequently, raw category medians in `price-ladder.csv` reflect the specific pricing of those three chains rather than a uniform census of South Jakarta independent cafés.
3. **Unverified Model Benchmarks (`S31`, `S46`):** CORSA's `Menu` sheet cites `S31` (*Common Grounds / Excelso / Burgreens / SaladStop menu benchmark*) and `S46` (*Jakarta specialty cafe pricing benchmark*), which are flagged as `Research` in `18-sources.csv` (`S31`, `S46`) and were not opened in `sources/sources.md` (where `REG-122` to `REG-126` only verify brand locations/websites, and `CMP-036` verifies specialty coffee prices from *Manual Jakarta*).

### 2. Four Structural Real-Estate Risks Masked by the Headline Rent Ratio

1. **The Ruko Multi-Floor Surface Distortion (`LB` vs. Ground-Floor Trading Area):**
   - In Jakarta property portals (`Rumah123`, `Lamudi`), a `150 m²` shophouse (`ruko`) in Senopati side streets, Kemang, or PIK is typically a **3-storey `4.5m x 11m` or `5m x 10m` building** (`~50 m²` per floor). Once stairs, ground-floor espresso bar, prep kitchen (`CAPEX (Rent)!C9` allocates `35 m²` to kitchen), and toilet are deducted, ground-floor seating is near zero, forcing guests up narrow stairs to Floor 2.
   - In contrast, a single-level `150 m²` mall or office-podium unit in SCBD stays on one plane. While `Inputs!E22` adds a `1.2x` GFA factor in the Conservative case (`180 m²`), the Base Case (`Inputs!F22 = 1.0`) assumes `150 m²` of rented area in a ruko delivers the same 55-seat single-level operational efficiency as an SCBD podium unit.

2. **Indonesian Upfront Lease Prepayment & Working-Capital Drain (`Inputs!E24:H29`):**
   - Unlike Swiss/European monthly commercial leases, Jakarta landlords—especially for rukos and independent street-front buildings—require **1 to 2 years of rent paid 100% upfront in cash upon signing** (`S13`, `Inputs!Q25`).
   - Model v0.6 correctly captures this in `CAPEX (Rent)!G117:G119`:
     - **Base Case (`Inputs!F25 = 12` months prepaid, `F27 = 2` months security deposit, `F29 = 2` months fit-out rent):** Upfront lease cash required before Day 1 is **IDR 604.39M** (`CHF 30,220`, `CAPEX (Rent)!H120`), representing **19.5% of total initial Lease-mode investment** (`IDR 3,098.75M`, `CAPEX (Rent)!G124`).
     - **Conservative Case (`Inputs!E25 = 24` months prepaid, `E27 = 3` months deposit, `E29 = 3` months fit-out rent, at P75 rent and `1.2x` GFA):** Upfront lease cash explodes to **IDR 2,107.62M** (`CHF 117,090`, `CAPEX (Rent)!F120`)—a **3.49x surge** over Base!

3. **Zero Empirical Footfall Validation (`Location!I8:N13`):**
   - The Location Scorecard (`Location!I7:O13`) defines five site-selection criteria (`Target Demo 25%`, `Foot Traffic 25%`, `Rent Value 20%`, `Competition 15%`, `Accessibility 15%`), but **every single score cell (`Location!I8:N13`) is blank**, producing a `0` weighted score (`Location!O8:O13`) and an explicit warning: `"Complete scoring ->"` (`Location!P8:P13`).
   - Thus, the `Demand Index` (`Location!H8:H13`: `1.00` SCBD/Senopati main, `0.95` PIK, `0.90` Senopati side, `0.85` Kuningan, `0.80` Kemang) is an **uncalibrated analyst prior (`S84`)**, not an empirically measured footfall index.

4. **Buy vs. Lease Asymmetry (`CAPEX (Buy)` vs. `CAPEX (Rent)`):**
   - Switching `Inputs!F18` from `2 (Lease)` to `1 (Buy)` replaces upfront lease prepayments (`IDR 604.39M` Base) with full strata/ruko acquisition at **IDR 25,000,000/m²** (`Inputs!F23`, `S01` Lamudi commercial average) plus `10.5%` buyer closing costs (`5%` BPHTB + `1.5%` AJB/notary + `0.5%` BPN + `3.5%` legal/due diligence, `CAPEX (Buy)!D116:D120`), adding **IDR 4,143.75M** (`CHF 207,188`) in property acquisition cost (`CAPEX (Buy)!G121`) and lifting Base total CAPEX from **IDR 3,098.75M** (`CHF 154,938`) to **IDR 5,093.75M** (`CHF 254,688`, `Scenarios!D46`). ### 2. Deep-Dive Audit by Subsystem: Internal Discrepancies & Economic Vulnerabilities

#### A. Revenue & Menu Engineering (`06-menu.csv` vs. `07-revenue.csv`) — Two Disconnected Revenue Engines
1. **Top-Down `Inputs` vs. Bottom-Up `Menu` Disconnect:**
   - The active revenue sheet (`Revenue!C5:C20`) does **not** pull item prices or COGS from `Menu` (`06-menu.csv`)! Instead, `Revenue` multiplies daily covers (`Inputs!H33 = 200`) by the three channel shares (`60%` dine-in, `25%` takeaway, `15%` delivery, `Inputs!H34:H37`) and three hard-coded channel net-spend assumptions (`Rp120,000` dine-in, `Rp55,000` takeaway, `Rp140,000` delivery, `Inputs!H36:H40`), yielding a channel-weighted average net spend of **Rp106,625/guest** (`Revenue!F7`) and monthly base F&B revenue of **IDR 639,750,000** (`Revenue!C13`), plus **IDR 15,000,000** events/catering (`Revenue!C17`) and **IDR 5,000,000** retail/merch (`Revenue!C18`) = **IDR 659,750,000/month** (`Revenue!C20`).
   - Meanwhile, the bottom-up `Menu` sheet (`Menu!A1:Q40`) builds a 20-SKU matrix totaling **177 items per 100 guests** (`Menu!F27 = 177`), which produces a bottom-up average net spend of **Rp103,375/guest** (`Menu!C31`, `-3.05%` below `Revenue!F7`), a bottom-up monthly F&B revenue of **IDR 620,250,000** (`Menu!G27`, `-IDR 19.50M/month` below `Revenue!C13`), and a bottom-up food/drink COGS of **25.2%** (`Menu!E31` / `I27`) vs. the **27.81%** raw F&B COGS used in `OPEX!D6` (`Inputs!H52:H53` weighted: `75% x 30% food COGS + 25% x 22% drink COGS = 28.0%` of base F&B revenue, or `27.81%` of total revenue including events/merch).
2. **Beverage Revenue Share Contradiction (`25.0%` in `Inputs!F41` vs. `41.1%` in `Menu!B33`):**
   - `Inputs!F41` sets `bev_share` (beverage attachment share of base revenue) at **25.0%** (`Revenue!C15:C16` splits the IDR 639.75M base revenue into **IDR 479,812,500 food [75%]** and **IDR 159,937,500 beverage [25%]**, and `OPEX!C6:C7` uses this exact `75%/25%` split to calculate Food COGS at `30%` and Beverage COGS at `22%`).
   - Yet in `Menu!B32:B33`, the bottom-up SKU mix (`55` pastas = `54.5%` of revenue; `82` drinks across rows `13–19` = **41.1% of revenue**; sides/breakfast/desserts = `17.3%`) relies on beverages generating **41.1%** of sales (`Rp42,490` per guest out of `Rp103,375`). If beverages actually represent `41.1%` of revenue (At `19.6%` bottom-up COGS, `Menu!I33`), blended gross margin is higher, **but** achieving `82` drinks per `100` guests alongside `55` pastas requires almost every dine-in pasta guest to order both a coffee/functional drink and a second item—an attachment assumption that has not been field-tested.

#### B. Staffing & Labor Compliance (`08-staffing.csv`) — Wage-Floor Compression & Single-Point-of-Failure Roster
1. **Roster Structure (`Staffing!A5:O26`):**
   - Base Case models **18 FTEs** (`Staffing!C21 = 18`: `1` Store Manager @ Rp14.0M, `1` Assistant Manager @ Rp8.5M, `1` Head Chef @ Rp11.0M, `3` Line Cooks @ Rp6.2M, `2` Kitchen Helpers/Dishwashers @ Rp5.4M, `1` Head Barista @ Rp7.2M, `3` Baristas @ Rp5.8M, `4` Service Crew/Runners @ Rp5.5M, `2` Cashiers/Hosts @ Rp5.6M; total base salary **IDR 127,000,000/month**, `Staffing!G21`).
   - Statutory and variable add-ons are properly modelled on top of base wages (`Staffing!G22:G26`):
     - **BPJS Ketenagakerjaan & Kesehatan (employer share):** **11.24%** (`Inputs!F60:F64`: JKK `0.54%` + JKM `0.30%` + JHT `3.70%` + JP `2.00%` + Kesehatan `4.00%` + `0.70%` buffer = **IDR 14,274,800/month**, `Staffing!G22`).
     - **THR (13th-month religious holiday allowance, Permenaker 6/2016):** **8.33%** (`1/12` accrued monthly = **IDR 10,583,333/month**, `Staffing!G23`).
     - **Service charge / tips pool:** **IDR 6,034,028/month** (`Staffing!G24`).
     - **Total Monthly Personnel Cost:** **IDR 157,892,162/month** (`CHF 7,895/mo`, `Staffing!G26`, or **23.93% of Base net revenue**).
2. **Minimum-Wage Violation in the Model (`2024/2025` vs. `2026/2027` UMP DKI Jakarta):**
   - `Inputs!F59` sets `min_wage` at **IDR 5,396,761/month** (`S14`, the **2025** DKI Jakarta UMP, verified in `DEM-041`). However, the verified **2026** DKI Jakarta UMP is already **IDR 5,729,876/month** (`+6.17%`, `P1C2-21` / `DEM-040`), and by CORSA's planned **July 2027** opening (`PnL_Monthly!C3`), at a `6.2%/yr` UMP growth rate (`Inputs!F15`), the 2027 UMP will be approximately **IDR 6,085,000/month**!
   - In `Staffing!D12:D18`, **four job categories totaling 11 of the 18 employees (`61%` of headcount) are paid below the verified 2026 UMP (Rp5.73M) and well below the projected 2027 UMP (~Rp6.09M)**:
     - Kitchen Helpers / Dishwashers (`2` FTE): **Rp5,400,000** (`Staffing!D12`) — barely above 2025 UMP (`Rp5.397M`), **Rp330k below 2026 UMP**, and **~Rp685k below projected 2027 UMP**.
     - Service Crew / Runners (`4` FTE): **Rp5,500,000** (`Staffing!D16`) — **below 2026 & 2027 UMP**.
     - Cashiers / Hosts (`2` FTE): **Rp5,600,000** (`Staffing!D18`) — **below 2026 & 2027 UMP**.
     - Baristas (`3` FTE): **Rp5,800,000** (`Staffing!D15`) — barely above 2026 UMP, **below projected 2027 UMP**.
     - Even in the Conservative column (`Staffing!C12:C18`), wages are set at **Rp5,400,000** across all four roles, which is **illegal for formal full-time employees in DKI Jakarta in 2026–2027**. Raising the floor of the bottom 12 operational staff (including Line Cooks) to a compliant 2027 structure (`~Rp6.1M–6.8M` base) adds `~IDR 10M–14M/month` in base + BPJS + THR payroll.
3. **Shift Coverage Vulnerability:** Operating 7 days/week from 07:00 to 22:00 (`15 hours/day = 105 operating hours/week`) under Indonesia's 40-hour statutory workweek (`UU Cipta Kerja / PP 35/2021`) requires `2.625` shift equivalents per station before vacation/sick leave (`~3.0x` with leave). Having only `1` Head Chef + `3` Line Cooks (`4` cooks total) and `2` Dishwashers across `105` weekly hours leaves **zero redundancy** during peak weekday lunch (`110+` pasta portions/day) if one cook is sick or takes annual leave.

#### C. OPEX & Aggregator Economics (`09-opex.csv`)
1. **Aggregator Commission Dilution (`OPEX!C22`, `Inputs!F37`, `Inputs!F48`):**
   - Base Case assumes `15%` of covers (`30` guests/day) order via GrabFood/GoFood/ShopeeFood at Rp140,000 net spend (`IDR 126.0M/month` delivery revenue, `Revenue!C12`), paying a `20%` platform commission (`Inputs!F48`). However, `OPEX!C22` computes delivery commission as **`3.0%` of total revenue (`IDR 19,792,500/month`)**, which actually corresponds to `15%` of **guest count** x `20%` commission (`0.15 x 0.20 = 3.0%`) rather than `20%` of actual **delivery revenue** (`20% x IDR 126.0M = IDR 25,200,000/month`, because delivery ticket `Rp140k` is `1.31x` the blended ticket `Rp106.6k`). This formula shortcut understates monthly aggregator commissions by **IDR 5,407,500/month** (`IDR 64.89M/year`) in the Base Case!
2. **Utility Tariffs (`OPEX!C17:C19` vs. Verified `REG-133`–`135`):**
   - `OPEX!C17` models electricity at **IDR 15,000,000/month** (`S15`, quoting PLN commercial tariff `Rp1,444–1,699/kWh`; `REG-135` verifies PLN B-2 tariff at **Rp1,444.70/kWh** in Q3 2026). At `Rp1,444.70/kWh`, Rp15.0M/month buys `10,383 kWh/month` (`~346 kWh/day` or `~23 kW` continuous load across 15h)—reasonable for espresso machines, HVAC, and refrigeration, **provided** pasta boilers and ovens run on LPG (`OPEX!C18` budgets **IDR 5,400,000/month** for water + gas). However, PAM Jaya commercial Group III/IV water tariffs (`REG-133`, Kepgub 730/2024) plus commercial non-subsidized 12kg/50kg LPG cylinders can easily push combined electricity + gas + water to `IDR 25M–30M/month` in a heavy-boiling pasta kitchen with Jakarta tropical HVAC loads (`REG-116`: year-round `27–28°C` mean temperature).

#### D. CAPEX (`10-capex-buy.csv` & `11-capex-rent.csv`) — Line-Item Mismatch Between Sheets & Under-Budgeted Working Capital
1. **Critical Formula Discrepancy Between `CAPEX (Buy)` and `CAPEX (Rent)`:**
   - Although `CAPEX (Buy)` (`10-capex-buy.csv`) and `CAPEX (Rent)` (`11-capex-rent.csv`) have identical unit quantities (`Col C`) and unit costs (`Col D:F`) across Sections 1–9 (`Rows 8–112`), **the Base (`Col G`) and Optimistic (`Col H`) subtotal formulas in `CAPEX (Buy)` do NOT multiply by Quantity (`Col C`), whereas `CAPEX (Rent)` DOES multiply by Quantity (`Col C x Col E`)!**
   - Specifically:
     - **Section 1 Fit-Out (`Row 8:17`):** In `CAPEX (Rent)!G8:G17`, `150 m²` x unit costs = **IDR 1,037,500,000** (`CAPEX (Rent)!G17`). In `CAPEX (Buy)!G8:G17`, the sheet simply sums the per-m² unit rates without multiplying by `150 m²`, outputting **IDR 56,550,000** (`CAPEX (Buy)!G17`)—understating fit-out in Buy mode by **IDR 980,950,000**!
     - **Section 2 Furniture (`Row 21:29`):** `CAPEX (Rent)!G29` = **IDR 213,500,000** (`Qty x Unit Cost`); `CAPEX (Buy)!G29` = **IDR 37,550,000** (unmultiplied unit rates), understating furniture in Buy mode by **IDR 175,950,000**!
     - The same error occurs in `CAPEX (Buy)` across Sections 3–5: `CAPEX (Buy)` Base subtotal before real estate (`Row 104/112`) is **IDR 950,000,000**, whereas `CAPEX (Rent)` Base subtotal before lease prepayments (`CAPEX (Rent)!G112`) is **IDR 2,494,360,000**! Thus, `CAPEX (Buy)!G125` (`IDR 5,093,750,000` = `IDR 950.0M + IDR 4,143.75M` land) is **missing IDR 1,544,360,000 of fit-out, furniture, and multi-unit kitchen equipment** in its Base and Optimistic columns (in Conservative `Col F`, `CAPEX (Buy)!F17` = `IDR 1,663.5M` *does* multiply by quantity, proving that `Col G` and `Col H` in `CAPEX (Buy)` have broken formulas!).
2. **Working Capital Cushion (`CAPEX (Rent)!G108:G110`):**
   - In `CAPEX (Rent)!G110`, Initial Inventory + Working Capital (`Rows 106–109`) is only **IDR 265,000,000** (`CHF 13,250`), comprising Rp45M F&B stock + Rp15M packaging + **Rp180M cash buffer (`CAPEX (Rent)!G108`)** + Rp25M pre-opening salaries (`CAPEX (Rent)!G109`).
   - Compare that **IDR 180M** cash buffer against the actual cumulative ramp-up burn in `PnL_Monthly`: in Month 1 (`50%` ramp, `PnL_Monthly!C29`), net loss is **-IDR 133.59M** (`EBITDA -IDR 87.75M`, `PnL_Monthly!C22`), and in Month 2 (`65%` ramp, `PnL_Monthly!D29`), net loss is **-IDR 67.26M** (`EBITDA -IDR 21.43M`). Even ignoring pre-opening delays, **pre-opening payroll for 18 staff over a 1-month training period costs ~IDR 140M, not IDR 25M (`CAPEX (Rent)!G109`)**, and any 2-month delay in ramp-up would exhaust the IDR 180M buffer immediately.

#### E. Funding & PT PMA Regulatory Capital (`12-funding.csv` & `Inputs!F87:F88`) — The BKPM IDR 10 Billion Gap

   - The 4 Swiss co-founders commit **CHF 50,000 each = CHF 200,000 total equity (`IDR 4,000,000,000` at the model's `20,000` FX rate, `Funding!B21`, or `IDR 4.32B` at `21,600` FX cited in `2026-10-09-corsa-finance-overview.md`)**, leaving an unallocated equity surplus of **IDR 1,366,062,500** (`CHF 68,303`, `Funding!B22`) in Lease mode.
2. **The BKPM / OSS-RBA PT PMA Investment Requirement (`Funding!A20:D28` & `REG-050`–`053`):**
   - Under BKPM Regulation No. 4/2021 and No. 5/2025 (`REG-050`–`053`, `Inputs!F87:F88`), a foreign-owned company (**PT PMA**) must satisfy **two distinct thresholds**:
     1. **Minimum Paid-Up Capital (`Modal Disetor`):** **IDR 2,500,000,000** (`Inputs!F87`, `Funding!B27`), which is **covered** (`1.00 = PASS`, `Funding!B28`) by the founders' CHF 200,000 (`IDR 4.0B–4.32B`) equity.
     2. **Minimum Total Investment Plan (`Nilai Investasi`):** **> IDR 10,000,000,000 per 5-digit KBLI per project location, EXCLUDING land and building (`di luar tanah dan bangunan`)** (`Inputs!F88`, `Funding!B24`, `REG-050`).
   - As `Funding!B25` explicitly calculates, CORSA's total capital (Founders' IDR 4.0B equity + IDR 0.465B debt = **IDR 4,464,812,500**) covers only **42.66% (`Funding!B25 = 0.4266`)** of the IDR 10B threshold (and in fact, eligible CAPEX excluding prepaid rent is only **IDR 2,494,360,000 = 24.9% of IDR 10B**!). Even if CORSA operates under two KBLI codes (`56101 Restoran` + `56303 Kedai Minuman` or `56304`), BKPM rules assess the `>IDR 10B` commitment per 5-digit KBLI / business line. Therefore, a single-unit IDR 3.1B Lease store **cannot legally register as a standalone PT PMA** without either: (a) committing to a multi-unit (`3–4` store) investment plan totaling `>IDR 10B` ex-land/building over the BKPM realization window (`Funding!D26`), or (b) structuring via a local nominee/franchise/management agreement (which carries severe legal governance risks).

#### A. What IS Broken by the 13-Month Truncation (`PnL_Monthly`, `Annual`, and half of `Dashboard`)
1. **`PnL_Monthly` (`14-pnl-monthly.csv`, `Columns C:O`):**
   - Although the sheet header (`PnL_Monthly!A1`) claims `"MONTHLY P&L PROJECTION (60 Months)"`, the sheet only contains **13 monthly columns**: `Col C` (`M1 Jul 2027`) through `Col O` (`M13 Jul 2028`), plus `Col P` (`Year 1 Total = SUM(C:N)`).
   - Months 1–6 apply the ramp-up schedule from `Inputs!F77:F82` (`50%`, `65%`, `75%`, `85%`, `92%`, `97%` of steady-state revenue), while food/drink COGS scale with revenue and fixed OPEX/staffing run at `100%`. Consequently, `PnL_Monthly!C22:H22` records monthly EBITDA of **-IDR 87.75M (M1)**, **-IDR 21.43M (M2)**, **+IDR 22.79M (M3)**, **+IDR 67.01M (M4)**, **+IDR 97.96M (M5)**, and **+IDR 120.07M (M6)**, reaching steady-state **+IDR 141.25M/month** in `M7–M13` (`PnL_Monthly!I22:O22`).
   - Across **Year 1 (`M1–M12`, `PnL_Monthly!P7:P29`)**, cumulative revenue is **IDR 6,854,802,500** (`CHF 342,740`), cumulative EBITDA is **IDR 925,494,307** (`CHF 46,275`, `13.50%` margin), cumulative Net Income is **IDR 333,962,796**, and end-of-Month-12 cumulative cash flow (`PnL_Monthly!N34`) is **-IDR 2,179,887,953** (rising to **-IDR 2,053,891,819** at the end of Month 13, `PnL_Monthly!O34`). Because cumulative cash flow is still `-IDR 2.05B` at M13 and `M14–M60` are missing, `PnL_Monthly` never crosses zero!
2. **`Annual` (`15-annual.csv`, `Annual!C5:G27`):**
   - `Annual!C5:C19` (**Year 1**) sums `M1–M12` correctly (`Revenue IDR 6,854.80M`, `EBITDA IDR 925.49M`, `Net Income IDR 333.96M`, `Free Cash Flow -IDR 2,173.25M` after `-IDR 3,098.75M` initial CAPEX in `Annual!C21`).
   - `Annual!D5:D19` (**Year 2**) sums `M13–M24` in `PnL_Monthly`—which captures **only Month 13 (`Jul 2028`)**! Hence Year 2 displays 1 single month of revenue (`IDR 659,750,000`), 1 month of EBITDA (`IDR 141,248,150`), and 1 month of FCF (`IDR 119,212,262`).
   - `Annual!E5:G23` (**Years 3, 4, and 5**) reference empty columns (`M25–M60`) and therefore evaluate to **exact zero (`0`) across every single line item** (`Revenue = 0`, `EBITDA = 0`, `FCF = 0`).
   - As a result, `Annual!C25:C27` computes a bogus 5-year Cumulative FCF of **-IDR 2,054,041,431** (`Annual!D24`), a bogus **5-Year NPV of -IDR 3,190,468,622** (`-CHF 159,523`, `Annual!C26` at `12%` WACC), and a bogus **5-Year IRR of `-46.9%`** (`Annual!C27`).

#### B. Why `Sensitivity` (`16-sensitivity.csv`) and `MonteCarlo` (`17-montecarlo.csv`) Are NOT Broken by `PnL_Monthly` — And Their Own Methodological Limits
1. **`Sensitivity` (`16-sensitivity.csv`, `A1:M49`) Is Completely Independent of `PnL_Monthly` and `Annual`:**
   - Inspection of `Sensitivity!B5:B13` confirms that the sensitivity baseline links directly to the active steady-state scenario column in `Scenarios` (`Scenarios!F6:F56`: `200` covers, `Rp106,625` spend, `IDR 659.75M` monthly revenue, `31.3%` COGS, `IDR 157.89M` staff, `IDR 63.25M` rent+SC, `IDR 141.25M` monthly EBITDA, `21.4%` margin, `IDR 3,098.75M` CAPEX).
   - Both the One-Way Tornado Table (`Sensitivity!A17:G25`, testing `-30%`, `-15%`, `Base`, `+15%`, `+30%` swings across 7 drivers) and the Two-Way Matrix (`Sensitivity!A29:I38`, crossing Daily Covers `80..300` against Net Spend `Rp70k..Rp160k`) compute **steady-state monthly EBITDA analytically from the `Scenarios` formula block**.
   - **Key Economic Insights from `Sensitivity!A17:I38`:**
     - **Top 2 Value Drivers (Equal #1 Rank):** Swinging **Daily Covers (`Sensitivity!A18`)** or **Avg Spend/Guest (`Sensitivity!A19`)** by $\pm 30\%$ (`140` to `260` covers, or `Rp74.6k` to `Rp138.6k` spend) shifts steady-state monthly EBITDA by **IDR 265.17M/month** (from **+IDR 8.66M/month** at `-30%` to **+IDR 273.83M/month** at `+30%`). A `-30%` drop in either volume (`140` covers/day) or ticket (`Rp74.6k`) nearly wipes out EBITDA (`1.3%` margin), confirming the `128 covers/day` break-even threshold (`Scenarios!D50`).
     - **Rank #3 — Food COGS % (`Sensitivity!A20`):** A $\pm 30\%$ swing in Food COGS rate (`21%` to `39%` vs `30%` base) shifts monthly EBITDA by **IDR 86.37M/month** (`+IDR 184.43M` to `+IDR 98.06M`).
     - **Rank #4 — Staff Cost (`Sensitivity!A21`):** A $\pm 30\%$ swing shifts monthly EBITDA by **IDR 76.20M/month** (`+IDR 179.35M` to `+IDR 103.15M`).
     - **Rank #5 — Rent (`Sensitivity!A22`):** A $\pm 30\%$ swing in base rent (`Rp38.5M` to `Rp71.5M/mo`) shifts monthly EBITDA by **IDR 33.00M/month** (`+IDR 157.75M` to `+IDR 124.75M`). Because SCBD base rent + SC is `9.6%` of Base revenue, **volume and ticket sensitivity (`IDR 265.2M` swing) is 8.0x larger than rent sensitivity (`IDR 33.0M` swing)**! This is a critical economic insight: paying a higher rent in SCBD (`Rp63.25M/mo`) vs. Kemang (`Rp18.06M/mo`, saving `Rp45.19M/mo`) is immediately justified if SCBD delivers at least `+35` incremental guests/day (`+17.5%` volume).
2. **`MonteCarlo` (`17-montecarlo.csv`, `A1:O1039`) Is Also Independent of `PnL_Monthly`, But Has Three Structural Caveats:**
   - **Independence from the 13-Month Bug:** `MonteCarlo!B5:B12` pulls its deterministic base directly from `Scenarios!D6:D56` (Base Case steady state) and runs `1,000` static steady-state iterations (`Rows 36–1035`). In each iteration, it draws 6 triangular random variables (`MonteCarlo!A16:F21`: Daily Guests `[110, 200, 270]`, Dine-in Spend `[105k, 120k, 140k]`, Takeaway Spend `[48k, 55k, 62k]`, Delivery Spend `[115k, 140k, 160k]`, Food COGS `[26%, 30%, 35%]`, Drink COGS `[18%, 22%, 26%]`) and 2 uniform random variables (`MonteCarlo!A22:F23`: Staff Multiplier `[0.90, 1.15]`, Rent Multiplier `[0.85, 1.25]`), computing:
     - Monthly Revenue (`Col J`), Monthly EBITDA (`Col K`), EBITDA Margin (`Col L`), Annual EBITDA (`Col M = 12 x Col K`), and **Simple After-Tax Steady-State Payback in Years (`Col N`)**, where `Payback = CAPEX / (Annual EBITDA x (1 - 22% CIT))` (`IDR 3,098.75M / (Col M x 0.78)`, or `99` if EBITDA $\le 0$).
   - **Summary of `MonteCarlo` Simulation Results (`MonteCarlo!A25:G32`, `n=1,000`):**
     - **Monthly Revenue:** Mean **IDR 648.62M**, Median (P50) **IDR 645.13M**, P10 **IDR 483.85M**, P90 **IDR 822.39M**, Min **IDR 350.09M**, Max **IDR 969.29M**.
     - **Monthly Steady-State EBITDA:** Mean **IDR 132.47M** (`20.4%` mean margin; P50 margin `20.2%`), Median (P50) **IDR 131.02M**, P10 **IDR 28.08M** (`5.8%` margin), P25 **IDR 75.50M** (`13.4%` margin), P75 **IDR 183.76M** (`24.8%` margin), P90 **IDR 246.23M** (`30.0%` margin), Min **-IDR 56.82M** (`-15.6%` margin), Max **+IDR 346.63M** (`36.9%` margin).
     - **Probability Indicators (`MonteCarlo!I26:N30`):**
       - `P(Monthly Steady-State EBITDA > 0)`: **95.3%** (`P(EBITDA < 0) = 4.7%`).
       - `P(Simple After-Tax Payback < 3 years)`: **68.0%**.
       - `P(Simple After-Tax Payback < 5 years)`: **81.1%** (Median P50 payback = **2.5 years**; P10 = **1.3 years**; P90 = **11.8 years**).
   - **Three Methodological Flaws in `MonteCarlo` to Flag for Phase 2:**
     1. **Contradictory Correlation Note (`MonteCarlo!B33` vs. `A34`):** Cell `MonteCarlo!B33` sets the correlation switch to `1` (`1 = correlated guests & spend (shared market factor, rho ~ 0.5)`), yet cell `MonteCarlo!A34` prints a static text note claiming `"Variables are drawn independently"` (`20-methods.csv` `M09` also repeats this).
     2. **Payback Formula Ignores Ramp-Up, Depreciation Tax Shield, and CAPEX Uncertainty:** `MonteCarlo!N36:N1035` holds initial CAPEX fixed at `IDR 3,098.75M` (ignoring the Conservative `IDR 5,798.49M` tail risk) and divides by `Annual Steady-State EBITDA x 0.78` (applying `22%` CIT directly to EBITDA rather than EBIT, while ignoring the ~IDR 550M Year-1 ramp-up deficit).
     3. **Unanchored Triangular Modes (`200` covers/day):** Because the triangular distribution for Daily Guests (`[110, 200, 270]`) is anchored on the unvalidated `200` covers/day assumption (`S44`, marked `Placeholder` in `Research_Tracker!G6`), the `95.3%` probability of positive EBITDA is conditional on true market demand centering near `193` guests/day (`(110+200+270)/3 = 193.3`). If true demand in an unvalidated location centers near the break-even point (`128` guests/day), `P(EBITDA > 0)` drops toward `~50%`.
